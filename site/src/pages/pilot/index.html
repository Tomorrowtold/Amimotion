So you know the effort before you say yes.

What you get

  • An anchor plan from a site survey
  • Tags and anchors for one bay or line
  • AmIHub on a PC in your plant, or in the cloud
  • A baseline before tags go on, and the same numbers after
  • A review of the results with your team

What you bring

  • One flow you want to see, such as trolleys between two stations
  • An owner on the floor who acts on what the map shows
  • Power at the anchor points, DC or PoE
  • The zones that matter, and the time limit for each

What your team does

  • Walks the site survey with our engineer
  • Fixes tags to the trolleys or tools in scope: adhesive, screw or cradle
  • Uses the live map and the wait times in the daily routine
  • Joins the review and decides on the numbers

Duration and price depend on the area, the tag count and the anchor plan, so the quote follows the survey. Anchors mount on columns and walls and need only power, with no data or sync cables to run.

Questions teams ask first.

What does an RTLS pilot involve?

An AmIMotion pilot covers one bay or line, a few zones, one question and one owner. It has a baseline before tags go on, an anchor survey and install, a review with the people who use it, then a decision.

How big should the first pilot be?

One main problem in one bounded area. Tag count, anchor plan and duration follow your site.

What if we can’t link jobs to carriers yet?

Start with trolley location and wait times. Make the job link a pilot step before calling any result job-level.

What should success mean?

Agree it up front: the right tags, useful position quality, fresh data, correct zones, and the effort for a person to respond.
