without the jargon.

The short answer

A real-time location system (RTLS) uses fixed receivers, called anchors, to work out where tagged objects are, usually indoors where GPS does not reach. A useful record has three parts: which object, where, and when it was seen. AmIMotion is a UWB RTLS: AmIGo tags, AmIMo anchors that locate them down to ±10 cm in 3D and AmIHub software.

Start with the object

A tag goes on a trolley or tool. Anchors pick up its signal, and software works out the position. The tag must sit on the right object, which is a different thing from knowing the job it carries.

Add the link to the job

Say trolley 14 holds job 208. That link comes from your workflow, with a start, an end and an owner.

Make time visible

Ask when each position was seen. A precise point can still be too old to act on.

Turn the map into a question

A zone, such as an assembly queue, gives a position meaning. Define what each zone means and who looks when something is off.

A short checklist

The right tag on the right object, position quality in your conditions, data age, how jobs are linked, and who responds. They tell you more than a moving dot on a demo screen.

Common follow-ups.

What does RTLS stand for?

Real-time location system.

Which radio does an RTLS use?

UWB, BLE, RFID, Wi-Fi or a mix. AmIMotion uses UWB, with BLE and Wi-Fi FTM on the same anchors.

Does RTLS work outdoors?

AmIMo anchors work indoors and in sheltered outdoor areas such as yards under a canopy.
