How to test accuracy.

UWB is usually accurate enough to tell neighbouring lanes and queues apart. AmIMotion quotes down to ±10 cm in 3D, with AmIMo anchors, and the real figure depends on the site. UWB’s very short pulses let the direct signal be separated from reflections off steel racking and forklifts, but a pilot should still measure accuracy in the hardest places on your floor.

  1. Agree what you measure
  2. Include the hard places
  3. Steel, racking and forklifts
  4. Measure data age separately
  5. End with a person

Agree what you measure

Ask what “typical” means for your setup, how true positions will be measured, and how the spread of errors is reported. Typical is the usual case, not every point. If your process needs a stricter figure, agree it in writing before the pilot.

Include the hard places

Test near metal, behind racking, at awkward mounting spots, near forklifts and with tags both moving and still. Note where coverage is expected and where the system should admit doubt.

Steel, racking and forklifts

Racking and machines bounce radio around. UWB’s wide band tells the direct path from the echoes, and AmIHub’s non-line-of-sight algorithms keep positions steady. Dense racking may still need more anchors, or anchors that see down each aisle.

Measure data age separately

Record when a position was seen, received and used. A fast system can still show old data if a tag reports slowly. Set a freshness target per task.

End with a person

Give a supervisor a real task. Can they find the right trolley and its job, and see how sure the system is? Record how much they had to correct.

Common follow-ups.

Is UWB accurate enough to track trolleys in a factory?

Usually, yes. AmIMotion locates tags down to ±10 cm in 3D, with AmIMo anchors, which is enough to tell neighbouring lanes and queues apart. Measure it on your floor in a pilot.

Does UWB work around steel racking and forklifts?

UWB’s very short pulses separate the direct signal from reflections off steel, and AmIHub adds non-line-of-sight handling. Dense racking can need extra anchors.

Does ±10 cm mean every reading is within 10 cm?

No. Down to ±10 cm is what AmIMo anchors reach in good conditions. A pilot reports the spread of errors on your floor.
