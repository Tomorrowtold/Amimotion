The short answer

An RTLS tag identifies the trolley, not the job on it. To know which job is on a trolley, the system needs a carrier-to-job association: a link set by a scan at loading or by your MES or ERP, with a start, an end and an owner. AmIMotion keeps that link in AmIHub next to each position, which is located down to ±10 cm in 3D, and shows “Job unknown” when there is no link.

One object, two identities

Trolley 14 has its own tag. Job 208 is what it carries now. Keeping them apart stops a reused trolley from dragging an old job forward.

Give the link a lifetime

A link starts and ends. Agree who starts and ends it, which event triggers the change, and how mistakes get corrected.

Respect the system of record

Your MES or ERP stays the authority on jobs and status. AmIHub supplies where and when. Agree the source of each field, so a location event never implies a business step.

Plan for the unknown

If there is no link, show the trolley’s location and say “Job unknown”. If a position is old, show it with its time. An honest partial answer beats an invented full one.

What to ask any vendor

Ask how jobs get linked, how links are kept and changed, which way data flows, and how mixed or split loads are handled. Then ask to see that workflow in a live demo.

Common follow-ups.

How do I know which job is on a trolley with RTLS?

Link the trolley’s tag to the job at loading, with a scan or from your MES. The link has a start and an end, and AmIHub shows it beside the trolley’s position.

Does AmIMotion track individual parts?

AmIMotion tags the carrier: the trolley, bin or rack. Which parts or job it holds comes from the carrier-to-job link, not from the radio.

What if one trolley carries several jobs?

Agree how mixed and split loads are linked before the pilot. One carrier can hold several links at once.
