How dwell time
is measured.

The short answer

Dwell time is the time a tagged carrier spends inside an agreed zone, from the moment it enters to the moment it leaves. A real-time location system measures it by time-stamping every zone entry and exit. AmIMotion does this with AmIGo tags located down to ±10 cm in 3D, so neighbouring queues and stations can be told apart and long waits on the shop floor stand out.

Start with the zones

A zone is an agreed area of the floor, such as an inspection queue or a dock lane. Draw zones around the process, not around the radio coverage. Neighbouring zones need enough positional precision to tell them apart, which is why UWB is used where queues sit side by side.

Entry, exit and the edge

Every time a tag crosses a zone boundary, the system records a time. Agree what counts as a visit: a short pass through a zone on the way somewhere else should not count as waiting. A small time or distance threshold at the edge stops a trolley parked on a boundary from flickering in and out.

What dwell includes

Dwell is not cycle time. It includes waiting, processing, breaks and anything else that happens while the carrier sits in the zone. To separate waiting from working you need another signal, such as a start or finish event from your MES or a scan.

Using it well

  • Set a time limit per zone and name who looks when it is passed.
  • Compare dwell by shift, product and station, not just averages.
  • Treat a long dwell as a reason to look, not as a diagnosis.

How AmIMotion does it

AmIHub time-stamps every enter, exit, idle and dwell event and keeps the history, so you can ask for time in zone by shift, station or time window. Events can be sent to your MES or ERP over MQTT, REST or WebSocket.

Common follow-ups.

How can I see where work in progress is waiting?

Tag the carriers, name the zones where work queues, and time every entry and exit. AmIHub shows how long each tagged carrier has waited in each zone.

Is dwell time the same as cycle time?

No. Dwell is time in a zone and includes waiting and processing. Cycle time needs process start and finish events.

Do we need an MES to measure dwell?

No. Dwell comes from location and time alone. The MES link adds which job was waiting.
