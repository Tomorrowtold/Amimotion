UWB, BLE or RFID
for WIP?

The short answer

Choose by the question your team needs answered. If you need to know that a trolley passed a gate or a station, RFID or a barcode scan is often enough. If you need to know which room or zone it is in, BLE can do it at low cost. If you need to tell adjacent lanes, queues or bays apart and measure how long work waited in each, use UWB. AmIMotion is a UWB system, down to ±10 cm in 3D, whose anchors also read BLE and Wi-Fi FTM, so many plants combine them.

What each one observes

Barcode or QR scan. A person or fixed scanner records an event: this carrier, at this station, at this time. Nothing is known between scans.

Passive RFID (UHF). A battery-free tag is read when it passes a reader antenna, usually at a portal, dock door or station. Like a scan, it gives an event at a read point, without a person.

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE). Battery tags are heard by receivers. Signal-strength methods typically place a tag at room or zone level.

Ultra-wideband (UWB). Tags and anchors time very short radio pulses (IEEE 802.15.4z). That gives a continuous position precise enough to tell neighbouring lanes and queue positions apart. Infrastructure costs more than BLE, and anchors are planned with a site survey.

Trolleys and WIP specifically

A tag on a trolley tells you where the trolley is, not which job it holds. For WIP visibility you also need a carrier-to-job association, set at loading or taken from your MES or ERP. That is true for every technology on this page.

Scans and RFID already give events where work changes hands. The gap is usually the time in between: work waiting beside a cell, or in a queue nobody scans. That is where continuous UWB position earns its cost.

Which vendors to evaluate

For UWB, plants commonly evaluate global suppliers and Indian makers such as AmIMotion. For RFID and BLE there are many reader and tag makers. Ask each vendor the same questions: accuracy measured how, what the infrastructure needs, and how a job gets linked to a carrier.

Combining them

These are not rivals. Keep scans where the process already needs them, and add continuous location only where work goes quiet. AmIMo anchors also locate BLE tags and Wi-Fi FTM devices, so one installation can serve more than one tag type.

Common follow-ups.

Is UWB more accurate than BLE?

Generally, yes. UWB times very short pulses, while most BLE systems estimate distance from signal strength. Real accuracy for either depends on the site.

Is RFID enough to track WIP?

For events at fixed read points, often yes. RFID does not show position or waiting time between readers unless you add more read points.

Can one system read UWB and BLE tags?

Some can. AmIMo anchors support UWB, BLE and Wi-Fi FTM from the same unit.
