AmIGo in short

AmIGo is AmIMotion’s UWB tag, built to IEEE 802.15.4z HRP on channels 5 and 9. With AmIMo anchors it is located down to ±10 cm in 3D. It reports 0.1 to 10 times a second when it moves and lasts 1 to 3 years on battery, typical and usage-dependent.

Where the battery life comes from.

A 3-axis accelerometer wakes the tag on motion and reports tilt and impact. At rest it deep-sleeps.

Battery level is reported to AmIHub, so replacements are planned, not discovered.

  • Whole groups of tags are set up from AmIHub
  • Vehicle-powered option for forklifts and tuggers
  • Several UWB positioning modes; confirm them for your hardware revision
  • The tag sends only when the accelerometer says it is moving.
  • Update rate is set per tag group, from 0.1 to 10 times a second.
  • 1 to 3 years on battery is typical. Busy tags at high rates use more.
  • Forklift and tugger tags can run off the vehicle instead.

Questions teams ask first.

What is an AmIGo tag?

AmIGo is AmIMotion’s UWB tag for trolleys, bins, tools, forklifts and badges. With AmIMo anchors it is located down to ±10 cm in 3D, and its battery lasts 1 to 3 years, typical.

What battery life does a UWB asset tag have?

An AmIGo UWB tag lasts 1 to 3 years on battery, typical. It depends on how often the tag moves and the update rate you set, because AmIGo sleeps when still.

Is AmIGo small enough for totes and hand tools?

It is a compact tag with adhesive and screw mounts, made for trolleys, bins, tools and fixtures. Exact dimensions are on the datasheet, shared under NDA.

Does every tag report at the same rate?

No. Update rate is set per tag group, from 0.1 to 10 times a second, and tags report only when they move.

Can AmIGo track people?

A badge form shows zone presence, under your site’s policy and with people’s consent. AmIMotion locates badges, not bodies or movements.
