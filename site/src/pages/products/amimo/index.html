AmIMo, in short

AmIMo is AmIMotion's UWB anchor. It records when each AmIGo pulse arrives and reads the angle it came from, in 3D, so fewer anchors fix a position. It keeps time with the other anchors over the air, sends its data to AmIHub over Wi-Fi, with an Ethernet option, and needs only power, from DC or PoE. Each anchor reaches about 50 m in clear line of sight. A site survey sets how many your floor needs.

How it locates a tag

Three radios. One anchor.

  • UWB

    AmIGo tags on carriers, tools and vehicles, down to ±10 cm in 3D with AmIMo anchors, depending on your floor.

  • BLE ranging

    Zone-level presence for BLE devices and beacons that carry no UWB tag.

  • Wi-Fi FTM

    Ranging with compatible Wi-Fi devices, from the same AmIMo anchors. Device support is confirmed for each site.

Questions about AmIMo.

What is an AmIMo anchor?

AmIMo is AmIMotion's UWB anchor. It times each tag pulse and reads its 3D angle of arrival. It offers BLE ranging and Wi-Fi FTM from the same unit, and needs only DC or PoE power, with no data or sync cable.

Does an RTLS anchor need data cabling?

Not AmIMo. It syncs over the air and sends its data to AmIHub over Wi-Fi, with an Ethernet option. Each AmIMo anchor still needs power, from DC or PoE.

How far does one AmIMo anchor reach?

About 50 m in clear line of sight, indicative and site-dependent. Racking, machines and steel shorten it, so a site survey sets the count.

How many AmIMo anchors does a floor need?

It depends on the accuracy you need, the ceiling height and how much racking blocks the view. Where the angle helps, as few as two anchors in view can fix a position. A site survey sets the count.

What happens if an anchor loses power?

AmIHub raises an alert when an AmIMo anchor goes dark, so your team knows which one to check.
