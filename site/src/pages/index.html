Here is how it knows.

A tag on what moves. A few anchors on your columns. Software that turns a radio pulse into a place and a time. Three parts, made by one team to work as one.

  • AmIGoThe tag. Rides on whatever carries the work.
  • AmIMoThe anchor. Listens from your columns.
  • AmIHubThe software. Turns pulses into answers.

AmIGo rides with the work.

Stick it on a trolley, a bin, a pallet, a forklift or a tool. While things stand still, it sleeps. When they move, it wakes and sends a short radio pulse. That habit is why one battery lasts one to three years.

  • 1 to 3 years per battery, depending on use
  • Versions for assets, vehicles and people
  • Reports its own battery level

AmIMo listens from the columns.

A few anchors go up on columns and walls, three to eight metres high. Each one notes the exact moment a pulse arrives, and the direction it came from. Anchors keep time with each other over the air, so all they need is power.

  • Mounted 3 to 8 m up
  • Power only, from DC or PoE
  • No data or sync cables to run

AmIHub turns pulses into places.

It weighs the timing and angle from every anchor in range and works out where the tag is, down to ±10 cm. Then it adds what only you know: your zones. Now a dot on a map is a trolley that reached the queue at 12:10 and is still there.

  • Down to ±10 cm in 3D, site-dependent
  • Every arrival and exit time-stamped
  • On a PC in your plant, or in the cloud
