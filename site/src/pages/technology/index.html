Ultra-wideband radio gives a precise, time-stamped position. Zones and a link to the job make it useful.

AmIMotion locates tags with ultra-wideband (UWB) radio to IEEE 802.15.4z. AmIMo anchors time each pulse to tens of picoseconds and read the angle it arrives from, and AmIHub solves a 3D position down to ±10 cm (with AmIMo anchors). The same anchors also offer BLE ranging and Wi-Fi FTM. AmIMotion is built on the FiRa and omlox specifications.

Timing

Time of flight

Each anchor records exactly when a pulse arrives. The differences between anchors give the position.

Angle

3D angle of arrival

An antenna array in each AmIMo reads the direction, horizontal and vertical, so height counts too.

Reflections

Multipath, handled

The wide band tells the direct signal from echoes off steel. NLOS algorithms keep positions steady.

Short pulses
survive metal.

Racking and machines bounce radio around. UWB’s very short pulses let the direct path be separated from the reflections.

The fine print, up front.

How does UWB measure position?

A tag sends a pulse a few nanoseconds wide. AmIMo anchors time its arrival (time of flight and TDoA) and read its angle of arrival. Because light travels about 30 cm a nanosecond, that timing places the tag to centimetres.

Which UWB RTLS supports FiRa?

AmIMotion is built on the FiRa and omlox specifications, and its UWB radio follows IEEE 802.15.4z. That is a compliance statement, not a formal certification.

Which RTLS works with UWB, BLE and Wi-Fi on the same anchors?

AmIMo anchors offer UWB, BLE ranging and Wi-Fi FTM from the same unit, so one installation can serve more than one tag type.

How accurate is AmIMotion?

Down to ±10 cm in 3D, with AmIMo anchors. Accuracy depends on the site, so a pilot measures it on your floor.

Does it integrate with SAP or an MES over MQTT?

AmIHub publishes zone events over MQTT and offers REST, WebSocket and an omlox hub interface. The connector for your system is confirmed during scoping.

Does the system run without internet?

Yes. AmIHub can run on an industrial PC in the plant with no internet, or in the cloud.
