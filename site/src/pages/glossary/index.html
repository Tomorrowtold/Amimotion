Glossary of location terms. Floor words and tech words, in plain English.

39 terms you will hear when people talk about location on a factory floor.

AmIMotion's glossary defines the words used in real-time location on factory floors, from RTLS and UWB to dwell time and carrier-to-job links. Each term has a plain definition, and where a term names an AmIMotion part, the definition says so. AmIMo anchors locate AmIGo tags down to ±10 cm in 3D, depending on the site.

Positioning

RTLS
Real-time location system: fixed infrastructure and software that continuously locate tagged objects, usually indoors where GPS does not work. AmIMotion is a UWB RTLS.
UWB
Ultra-wideband: radio that sends very short pulses across a wide band of frequencies. Short pulses allow precise timing, so distance can be measured to centimetres and echoes told apart.
Tag
The small device fixed to whatever is tracked: a carrier, tool or vehicle. AmIMotion's tag is AmIGo, which wakes on motion and lasts 1 to 3 years on its battery, typical and usage-dependent.
Anchor
A fixed device at a known position that receives UWB signals from tags. AmIMotion's anchor is AmIMo, a UWB anchor with a 3D angle-of-arrival antenna array. AmIMo needs power from a DC adapter or PoE, and no data or sync cabling.
Time of flight
The time a radio signal takes to travel from transmitter to receiver. Multiplied by the speed of light, about 30 cm per nanosecond, it gives distance.
TWR
Two-way ranging: tag and anchor exchange messages and measure the round trip to work out the distance.
TDoA
Time difference of arrival: a position worked out from the differences in when one signal reaches several synchronised anchors.
UL-TDoA
Uplink TDoA: the tag sends a short blink and the anchors work out its position. Tags only transmit, so many low-power tags can be tracked.
DL-TDoA
Downlink TDoA: anchors transmit and the device works out its own position, like GPS indoors.
AoA
Angle of arrival: an antenna array measures the direction a signal comes from. 3D AoA measures the horizontal and the vertical angle.
Multipath
Copies of a signal that arrive late after bouncing off metal, walls or racking.
NLOS
Non-line-of-sight: the direct path between tag and anchor is blocked, so the strongest signal may be a reflection.
Over-the-air sync
Keeping anchor clocks in step by radio instead of timing cables. AmIMo anchors sync over the air.
Wi-Fi FTM
Fine Timing Measurement (IEEE 802.11mc): Wi-Fi round-trip timing that lets compatible Wi-Fi devices estimate distance without a separate tag.
BLE ranging
Estimating distance to Bluetooth Low Energy devices. Usually coarse, at room or zone level.
Update rate
How often a tag reports. 0.1 Hz is once every 10 seconds; 10 Hz is ten times a second.
Site survey
A visit that maps ceiling height, racking, metal and sight lines before anchors go up. AmIMotion sets the final anchor count from a site survey.
PoE
Power over Ethernet: power delivered over a network cable. An AmIMo anchor can take its power by PoE or from a DC adapter.

Standards

IEEE 802.15.4z
The UWB standard amendment that adds enhanced and secure ranging. HRP is the high-rate pulse physical layer used by most current UWB chips.
FiRa
The FiRa Consortium, an industry body that defines interoperable UWB specifications and runs certification programmes.
omlox
An open standard for interoperable industrial location systems, so different location technologies and vendors can feed one location hub.
Certification
A formal certification means an authorised body has tested a product against a specification. Following a specification is a company's own statement. AmIMotion describes itself as built on the FiRa and omlox specifications.

Operations

WIP
Work in progress: partly finished goods moving through production.
WIP visibility
Knowing where work in progress physically is, which job each carrier holds and how long it has waited in each zone.
Carrier
The trolley, bin, rack or container that holds WIP. In a WIP visibility system the carrier carries the tag.
Association
The link between a tagged carrier and the job it holds, which gives a location its work meaning. A link needs a start, an end and an owner.
Zone
An agreed area of the floor, such as an assembly queue. Entering or leaving it creates a time-stamped event.
Geofence
A boundary drawn on the floor map. Crossing it creates an event, in the same way as a zone.
Dwell
Time spent in a zone, from entry to exit. Dwell includes waiting, processing and breaks, so it is not the same as cycle time.
Exception
A case that asks a person to look, such as work that has waited longer than the team agreed. An exception is not a diagnosis.
Last seen
The last place and time a tag was located. An honest map shows a quiet tag as last seen, never as live.
Freshness
How old a position is when someone uses it. A precise position can still be too old to act on.
MES, ERP, WMS
Manufacturing execution, enterprise resource planning and warehouse management systems: the systems of record for jobs, orders and stock.
AGV and AMR
Automated guided vehicle, which follows fixed paths, and autonomous mobile robot, which navigates freely.
Milk run
A fixed delivery loop, often by tugger, that feeds line-side stations.

Software and AI

AmIHub
AmIMotion's software. AmIHub works out positions and keeps the live map, zones, time-stamped events and interfaces: REST, MQTT, WebSocket, an omlox hub interface and a built-in MCP server. AmIHub runs on a PC inside your plant with no internet dependency, or in the cloud.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: an open standard that lets AI assistants use external tools and data. AmIHub has a built-in MCP server.
Industrial spatial intelligence
A time-stamped location for what moves in a plant, joined with context such as jobs and zones, so people, systems and AI assistants can answer where work is and where it waits.
Edge
Computing done on site, near the equipment, rather than in a remote cloud.
