Positioning

RTLS
Real-time location system: fixed infrastructure and software that continuously locate tagged objects or people, usually indoors where GPS does not work.
UWB
Ultra-wideband: radio that sends very short pulses across a wide band of frequencies. Short pulses allow precise timing, so distance can be measured to centimetres and echoes told apart.
Tag
The small device attached to whatever is tracked: a carrier, tool, vehicle or badge. AmIMotion’s tag is AmIGo.
Anchor
A fixed reference device at a known position that receives or sends UWB signals. AmIMotion’s anchor is AmIMo, with a 3D angle-of-arrival antenna array.
Time of flight
The time a radio signal takes to travel from transmitter to receiver. Multiplied by the speed of light, about 30 cm per nanosecond, it gives distance.
TWR
Two-way ranging: tag and anchor exchange messages and measure the round trip to compute distance.
TDoA
Time difference of arrival: position computed from the differences in when one signal reaches several synchronised receivers.
UL-TDoA
Uplink TDoA: the tag sends a short blink and anchors compute its position. Tags only transmit, so many low-power tags can be tracked.
DL-TDoA
Downlink TDoA: anchors transmit and the device computes its own position, like GPS indoors, which helps privacy.
AoA
Angle of arrival: an antenna array measures the direction a signal comes from. 3D AoA measures horizontal and vertical angle.
Multipath
Copies of a signal that arrive late after bouncing off metal, walls or racking.
NLOS
Non-line-of-sight: the direct path between tag and anchor is blocked, so the strongest signal may be a reflection.
Over-the-air sync
Synchronising anchor clocks by radio instead of timing cables.
Wi-Fi FTM
Fine Timing Measurement (IEEE 802.11mc): Wi-Fi round-trip timing that lets compatible Wi-Fi devices estimate distance without a separate tag.
BLE ranging
Estimating proximity to Bluetooth Low Energy devices; coarse and typically zone-level.
Update rate
How often a tag reports per second. 0.1 Hz is once every 10 seconds; 10 Hz is ten times a second.

Standards

IEEE 802.15.4z
The UWB standard amendment that adds enhanced and secure ranging. HRP is the high-rate pulse physical layer used by most current UWB chips.
FiRa
The FiRa Consortium, an industry body that defines interoperable UWB specifications and runs certification programmes.
omlox
An open standard for interoperable industrial location systems, so different location technologies and vendors can feed one location hub.
Compliant
A company states that its product follows a specification. A formal certification means an authorised body has tested it. AmIMotion describes itself as built on the FiRa and omlox specifications.

Operations

WIP
Work in progress: partly finished goods moving through production.
WIP visibility
Knowing where work in progress physically is, which job each carrier holds and how long it has waited in each zone.
Carrier
The trolley, bin, rack or container that holds WIP. In a WIP visibility system the carrier carries the tag.
Association
The time-bounded link tag to carrier to job that gives a location its work meaning. It has a start, an end and an owner.
Zone
An agreed area of the floor, such as an assembly queue. Entering or leaving it creates a time-stamped event.
Dwell
Time spent in a zone, from entry to exit. It includes waiting, processing and breaks, so it is not the same as cycle time.
Exception
A rule breach, such as dwell above an agreed limit, that asks a person to look. It is not a diagnosis.
MES, ERP, WMS
Manufacturing execution, enterprise resource planning and warehouse management systems: the systems of record for jobs, orders and stock.
AGV and AMR
Automated guided vehicle, which follows fixed paths, and autonomous mobile robot, which navigates freely.
Milk run
A fixed-route delivery loop, often by tugger, feeding line-side stations.

Software and AI

AmIHub
AmIMotion’s software: it works out positions and keeps the live map, zones, time-stamped events and interfaces.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: an open protocol that lets AI assistants call external tools and data. AmIHub has a built-in MCP server.
Industrial Spatial Intelligence
Precise, time-stamped location of what moves in a plant, joined with context such as jobs and zones, so people, systems and AI can answer where work is and where it waits.
Edge
Computing done on site, near the equipment, rather than in a remote cloud.
