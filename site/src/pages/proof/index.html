How we prove it. On your floor, before any claim.

Every figure on this site comes with its condition. A pilot measures the rest on your floor, against a baseline you agree first.

AmIMotion proves its value on your own floor. A pilot starts in one bay or one line. Before any tag goes on, your team agrees a baseline: how long work waits and how long people search today. The pilot then measures the same things with AmIGo tags, AmIMo anchors and AmIHub. Accuracy is tested where your floor is hardest. The published figure is ±10 cm in 3D with AmIMo anchors, typical and site-dependent, and every figure carries its condition like that one.

Three kinds of proof.

  • A baseline first.

    Agree what to measure, and measure it, before any tag goes on. Then the pilot compares like with like. Measure before you claim.

  • Accuracy, tested on site.

    Test where your floor is hardest: steel, racking and tight aisles. Accuracy depends on the site, so it is measured in the pilot. How accurate is UWB?

  • Figures with conditions.

    Every published figure for AmIGo tags, AmIMo anchors and AmIHub sits next to the condition it holds under. Specifications.

What a pilot shows.

A pilot follows one flow in one bay. It shows where each tagged trolley is, how long it waited in each zone, and which job it carried. Your team reads the result against the baseline it agreed. How a pilot runs.

The pilot tags trolleys, bins and pallets, not people. People and privacy.
