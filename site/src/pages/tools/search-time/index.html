Time spent searching. Your own numbers.

How many people look, how often and for how long. The calculator multiplies it out. It assumes nothing about savings.

AmIMotion's search-time calculator multiplies five of your own figures: people who search each shift, searches per person, minutes per search, shifts a day and working days a year. It gives hours a year, hours a day and the same time as full-time people, at 8 hours a day. Add a loaded hourly cost to see it in rupees. It shows what searching costs you today. It does not forecast a saving.

Where the numbers come from.

  • Your figures

    The sliders start at 12 people, 4 searches, 8 minutes, 2 shifts and 300 days. Those are only starting positions, not industry averages. Move them to match your floor.

  • The sum

    People, times searches, times minutes, times shifts, times days, divided by 60, gives hours a year. Full-time people divides that by 8 hours for each working day.

  • The cost

    If you add a loaded cost per hour, the tool multiplies the hours by it. Time freed is not the same as money saved, so read it as the cost of searching today.

Count it for real.

Most plants estimate these numbers. A pilot counts them. Before it starts, time how long it takes to find the trolleys in one flow.

Then AmIMotion tags them. An AmIGo tag rides on each trolley, and AmIHub's live map shows where it was last seen, and when. You find out from a screen, not a walk.

Time the same searches again with the map open. That difference, on your floor, is the number you can defend.

Questions teams ask first.

How much time do teams spend searching for trolleys and tools?

AmIMotion does not publish an average, because it varies too much from floor to floor. Enter your own figures here, then measure them in a pilot before you rely on them.

Does the calculator include savings?

No. It shows what searching costs today. Any change has to be measured on your own floor, against a baseline.

Does my data leave the page?

No. The calculator works in your browser and keeps nothing.
