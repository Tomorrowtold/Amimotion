How the estimate works.

Three assumptions, all of them this page's own.

  • A grid over your floor

    The tool lays a square grid over the floor and counts its corners. The grid is about 25 m for an open hall, 18 m with some racking and 12 m with dense high racking. These spacings are planning assumptions, not a figure from an AmIMotion datasheet.

  • A range, not a number

    It shows a fifth under and a quarter over that count, so you can plan a budget around it. It never gives fewer than 4 anchors for 3D or 3 for 2D.

  • Tall halls

    Above 10 m, the grid closes by a tenth. AmIMo anchors are recommended at 3 to 8 m up, so a tall hall is where the survey matters most.

What the survey adds.

Each AmIMo anchor reaches about 50 m around it in clear line of sight. Racking and steel change that, so we survey your floor and set the count.

An AmIMo anchor records when each pulse arrives and the angle it came from. Time and angle together can mean fewer anchors than timing alone.

Each anchor needs only power, from DC or PoE. It syncs over the air and sends its data to AmIHub over Wi-Fi, so there is no data or sync cable to run. Ethernet is also an option.

With these anchors, AmIHub places a tag down to ±10 cm. How close depends on your floor.

Questions teams ask first.

How many anchors does a UWB system need?

It depends on the floor. Each AmIMo anchor reaches about 50 m in clear line of sight, but racking, steel and ceiling height change that. AmIMotion sets the final count from a site survey. This tool gives a first range to plan a budget around.

Do AmIMo anchors need data cables?

No data or sync cable. Anchors sync over the air and send their data to AmIHub over Wi-Fi. They need only power, from DC or PoE. Ethernet is also an option.

How high do anchors go?

On a column or wall, 3 to 8 m up is recommended. Specifications.
