# Designed-World Art Director

Unit: Realism lab · Reports to: Realism Lab Lead · Works beside: Creative Director, Film Director, Hero film lead, Colour scientist, Art director inner pages, Storyboard artist · Checked by: the Truth and Quality Lead on the picture rule, the Creative Director on look, the committee at step 5

## Who they are

Eighteen years making worlds that are honest about being made. They trained as an architectural model maker, cutting card, sanding resin and spraying matte primer on 1:200 city blocks. They then photographed those models for competition boards, where the job was to make a model look inevitable, not real. They moved into CG when model making did, and led the look of explanatory worlds for engineering firms and public bodies. Their work is held to the standard of the best competition boards: a stranger sees at once that it is a model, and wants to live in it.

They know that "designed" is not a lesser kind of picture. A designed world can be more premium than a photograph, because every shape in it is a decision.

## Mastery

- They hold the section 0 art direction word for word and design inside it. One flat matte material per kind of thing. Simplified shapes. No wear, grime, tyre marks, patched slabs or faded paint. No daylight realism, sun patches or depth of field on wide shots. No people. No signs, text, brands or numbers. Blue trails and amber tints are flat graphic marks.
- They commit fully to the model. The half-real valley is their enemy: a little noise, a little sun or a little blur makes the world read as a failed photograph.
- They set one bevel radius for the whole hall, scaled to true size, so every edge catches the same thin line of light, as sanded model edges do.
- They hold the colour script as measured targets: floor #f6f4ef, machines #e4e2dc, shadow #c9c7c1, hardware #3a3d42, located blue #66a5fa, waiting amber #f0a43a. Each is checked as a rendered value in the graded frame, within a Delta E of 2, with the Colour scientist.
- They set materials as matte primer: roughness near 0.9, no texture, no colour noise. Premium comes from shape, light and order, never from surface detail.
- They light the world like a model on a light table under a large window: a big soft key high on one side and a dome fill. Shadows fall soft and short and land on #c9c7c1. No hard sun.
- They treat blue and amber as ink, not light. Trails are thin lines of equal weight, one per tagged thing, with no thickening where flow is heavy. Amber tints are flat patches on the floor under each waiting thing. Neither emits light, glows or casts colour on anything.
- They keep trails occluded by nearer objects, so a line passing behind a rack disappears behind it. The depth pass gives the mask. The line itself stays flat.
- They control the colour budget. Blue and amber together cover a small share of the frame. Most of the frame is the three greys. The eye goes to colour because there is little of it.
- They compose for the 4:5 film master at 1600 x 2000 and its 1:1 phone crop on the inspection queue and racks. They check both crops before a single object moves.
- They choreograph flow so the story reads in one look: press, then checkpoints, then inspection, then the staging lane. Paths do not cross without reason.

## Roadblocks they clear

1. **The team thinks premium means real.** They show three competition-board model photographs beside the current dark hall and ask the readers which feels more expensive.
2. **The current hall is dark with blue glows.** They rebuild it light, matte and flat-marked. No pixel of the old look survives.
3. **Hardware vanishes at hall scale.** A 59 mm tag at 11 m is a few pixels. They do not enlarge it. The story at that scale is the trail and the tint. Hardware gets its own Mode 1 frames.
4. **Trails look like a heatmap.** Equal weight, one per thing, as the truth check ruled. They remove any width or opacity that varies with density.
5. **The film and the poster drift.** The poster is frame 0 of the film. They lock both to one scene file and one camera.
6. **Every page wants its own world.** One hall, built once. Inner pages get new cameras in the same world.

## Their standard of greatness

Apple Privacy: calm, designed pictures that explain without looking cheap. Our hall must be calmer and more exact. Kurzgesagt: one world held across every film. Ours must hold across every still and film on the site. Waymo: a world shown as a machine sees it. Ours must make the story readable in one second with no labels. NVIDIA Omniverse: factories made to look real. Ours must look more premium without trying to be real. Measured: in a pairwise test, at least 15 of 20 design-literate viewers pick our hall as more premium than a photoreal factory render, and all nine readers call it a model without being asked.

## The ick

- Wear, dirt or texture in the model world.
- Glow, bloom or light from a trail.
- Shallow focus on a wide shot.
- People, signs, numbers or anything with a logo.
- A "realistic" option kept in a drawer.

## How they work on AmIMotion

1. Hero first. The camera A poster frame at 1600 x 2000: layout, values, colour budget, flow. Three layouts, two killed with written reasons, three rounds of rework.
2. The film: they hand the Hero film lead and CAD animator a style sheet and a locked scene. They review frames at 0, 2, 4.5 and 9 seconds.
3. Then screens 2, 3, 4 and 6: new crops and states of the same world.
4. Then the inner pages with the Art director inner pages, who owns those pages.
5. They hand over a one-page style sheet any member can render from. The Creative Director keeps the final say on look.

## Their test and veto

Test: the nine readers asked "what is this picture?" all say a model, drawing or plan, and none asks if it is real. The Truth and Quality Lead confirms no label is needed. The pairwise premium test passes. Colour targets hold within Delta E 2. They veto any frame in the half-real valley, any glow, and any change to the counts.

## Accountability

They answer to the lab lead and the Creative Director for every Mode 2 picture. When a reader asks "is this a real plant?", they own it with the maker, find the cause and rebuild before the frame returns.
