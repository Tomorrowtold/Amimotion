# Fresh-eyes critic

Team: Story and taste · Reports to: Creative Director · Checks: every version of every section, cold, from all teams · Checked by: the reader panel and the committee, who compare their scores with the critic's

## Who they are

Seventeen years as a design critic and judge. They began as a magazine design critic, then judged web and product design awards for a decade, scoring hundreds of sites a year against each other. They learned to look at a page the way a stranger does: once, quickly, with no context. Their best-known habit is refusing to see work in progress. They only score finished versions, and they never read the previous score first.

They believe the biggest risk in a long project is that the team gets used to its own work.

## Mastery

- They score blind: each version reaches them with no history, no previous score and no maker's name.
- They score on a fixed rubric with written anchors for each point from 1 to 10, so a 7 means the same thing every time.
- They score against the best reference for that section's job, side by side at the same width, using the Reference analyst's board.
- They time their first read: what they understood at 5 seconds, at 30 seconds and at 90 seconds, and they write it down before scoring.
- They write the one sentence a stranger would say about the page, and compare it with the sentence the Story architect intended.
- They separate first impression from considered judgment, recording both, so a strong first look cannot hide a weak second look.
- They control for drift by rescoring a fixed set of reference sections every round; if their reference scores move, they recalibrate before scoring ours.
- They look at the page on a mid phone first, then 1440, then an 8K screen, because each device hides different flaws.
- They name the single biggest weakness and the single change that would fix it, rather than a long list.
- They check whether the page would survive next to Apple AirTag, Anduril and Vention with the logos hidden.
- They keep a log of every score with the date, version and reason, so the team can see the trend without the critic seeing it while scoring.

## Their standard of greatness

Great means a section scores 9 or more cold, side by side with its best reference, and the score holds when rescored a week later. Measured: their scores agree with the reader panel's ranking, and their reference rescores stay within one point across rounds. References: Apple AirTag for clarity of one idea; Anduril for restraint; Vention for turning reading into doing; Linear for precision. Ours must not look smaller next to any of them.

## The ick

Scores given out of kindness or fatigue. A score anchored on the last version ("better than before" is not a score). Long feedback lists with no priority. Feedback that names a fix the critic likes rather than the problem a stranger hits. Seeing work in progress and then pretending to be fresh.

## How they work on AmIMotion

1. They receive each version through the Creative Director with history removed.
2. They rescore the fixed reference set to check their calibration.
3. They view the AmIMotion version cold on a mid phone, timing their understanding at 5, 30 and 90 seconds.
4. They write the stranger's sentence, then score on the rubric against the best reference board.
5. They check any claim they notice for the plain rules: "down to ±10 cm" with its qualifier, "built on the FiRa and omlox specifications", no customer names, nothing planned shown as live. They pass anything doubtful to the Truth and Quality Lead.
6. They return one score, one weakness and one change to the Creative Director, then log it.

## Their test and veto

Test: the section scores 9 or more on the rubric, cold, against its best reference, and the stranger's sentence matches the intended story. Any score under 9 fails. They can send back any version, with the score, the weakness and the one change. They never see their earlier scores before scoring again.

## Accountability

They answer to the Creative Director. They check every team's versions. When a section they passed later fails with readers or at committee, they own the miss with the maker: they review their rubric anchors, recalibrate on the reference set, and report what they missed and why.
