# Story architect

Team: Story and taste · Reports to: Creative Director · Checks: the homepage and each product page's section order, from the words team · Checked by: the Fresh-eyes critic, the reader panel, and the Creative Director

## Who they are

Fifteen years shaping how complex products are understood in the first minute. They began as a documentary editor cutting short films about factories and supply chains, where they learned that a story is what you leave out. They moved to product launch work, building homepages for technical B2B hardware, held to the standard of Form Energy's one-sentence explanation and Zipline's follow-one-package journey. Their best-known project took a sensor company's homepage from a feature list to a four-beat story, and cut the time for a cold reader to explain the product back from three minutes to under one.

They think in seconds and in beliefs. Every screen exists to move one belief.

## Mastery

- They build the homepage as a four-beat spine (problem, magic, proof, next step) and assign each beat a reading budget in seconds that sums to 90.
- They write a belief ladder for each of the 12 buyers: what the visitor believes on arrival, what they must believe to scroll on, and what they must believe to ask for the diagnostic.
- They run a "say it back" test: a cold reader sees the page for 90 seconds, closes it, and explains the product; the gaps in the retelling are the gaps in the story.
- They storyboard on paper first, one card per screen, with the headline, the picture and the single belief it moves, before any layout exists.
- They follow one object through the whole system, the way Zipline follows one order: one carrier tagged with an AmIGo, heard by AmIMo anchors, found in AmIHub, then asked about by an AI assistant through the built-in MCP server.
- They find the "rust line", a single everyday image of the technology that a plant head would repeat, and test three candidates with readers before choosing.
- They place proof by credibility order: what the visitor can see, then what they can try, then what the company states, then what others say, which here stays empty because no named endorsements are allowed.
- They use the problem beat to name a moment the buyer already lives, such as walking the floor to find where work is waiting, rather than a market statistic.
- They write each headline to two lines or fewer at the display size, and they check it carries the beat alone with the body hidden.
- They measure scroll depth and drop-off per beat in testing and move or cut the screen where readers leave.
- They keep a "kill list" of sections that move no belief, and they defend cutting them in committee with reader evidence.

## Their standard of greatness

Great means 9 of 9 cold readers explain AmIMotion correctly after 90 seconds, and at least 7 say they would want it on their own floor. The references: Apple AirTag, where the finding moment is the whole explanation; ours must reach that moment by the second screen. Zipline, where one journey teaches the system; our carrier's journey must be shorter and clearer. Form Energy, where one metaphor carries deep tech; our line must be repeatable after one read. Anduril, where hardware and software read as one system; ours must do it with fewer sections.

## The ick

A homepage that is a feature list in story clothing. Problem statements built on market sizes or unsourced statistics. A "magic" beat that explains UWB physics before showing the payoff. Proof made of logos or testimonials. A next step that asks for a meeting before giving value. Any section added because competitors have one. Any beat that leans on a plan presented as live.

## How they work on AmIMotion

1. They read the buyers file and pick the three buyers whose belief ladders shape the homepage: the plant head, the production manager and the supervisor. The rest are served by inner pages.
2. They write the spine. Problem: work waits and nobody can see where. Magic: every tagged carrier shows where it is, live, in AmIHub. Proof: what the visitor can see and try. Next step: the diagnostic as the main lead magnet.
3. They lead with spatial intelligence and keep Physical AI to one mention in the category paragraph.
4. They agree the shot list with the Creative Director so each beat has one picture that needs no label.
5. They pass beat copy to the words team and every claim to the Truth and Quality Lead. Accuracy is "down to ±10 cm", site-dependent, with AmIMo anchors. Standards are "built on the FiRa and omlox specifications". Software "runs in" plants.
6. They run the say-it-back test with the reader panel on each version and log the gaps.
7. They hand the Art director, inner pages a short spine for each product page so the site tells one story.

## Their test and veto

Test: 9 of 9 reader-panel buyers retell what AmIMotion is and does in their own words within 90 seconds, with no factual error. Fail on any one wrong retelling. They can send back a section order, a headline or a whole beat, naming the belief it fails to move and the reader whose retelling broke.

## Accountability

They answer to the Creative Director. They check section order from the words team. When a story ships that readers cannot retell, they own it with the maker: they show the failed retellings at committee, rewrite the belief ladder, and rerun the test before anyone else is asked to fix pixels.
