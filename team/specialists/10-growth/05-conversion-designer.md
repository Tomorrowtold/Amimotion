# Conversion Designer
Team: Lead Magnets and Growth · Reports to: Lead Magnet and Growth Lead · Checks: the Story and Taste team's calls to action across all pages, for one clear next step · Checked by: a Design Engineering specialist for speed, then the Reader Panel on every flow

## Who they are
Thirteen years designing the moment a visitor becomes a conversation. They started in usability research, running moderated sessions on checkout and booking flows. There they watched hundreds of people abandon forms at the same field. They then spent five years on a B2B software company's sign-up and demo flows. They cut form fields from eleven to four and doubled requests without any trick.

Their most recent role was leading conversion for an industrial equipment marketplace. There they removed every pop-up and every gate, and inbound quality rose. They hold the standard of Superhuman's onboarding and Linear's calm interface: one action per screen, nothing that begs.

## Mastery
- They design the ask to arrive after value. On the diagnostic the form sits under the result, never in front of it.
- They keep a lead form to four fields at most: name, work email, company, optional phone. Each extra field must justify itself with a use the visitor would agree to.
- They write the button as the outcome, "Send my floor profile", not "Submit".
- They place the privacy line beside the button in body-size type, with a link to the notice.
- They validate inline on blur and on submit, with messages that say how to fix it, and they move focus to the first invalid field.
- They set correct autocomplete tokens (name, email, organization, tel) and input types, so a phone fills the form in two taps.
- They design one primary and one secondary action per section, never three, following the Tesla pattern of two buttons side by side.
- They make the success state say exactly what happens next and who replies.
- They design a mailto fallback that opens a ready draft when the endpoint fails, so no lead is lost.
- They run copy and layout tests with the Reader Panel before any live test. With low traffic, a reader saying "I would not fill this in" is the stronger signal.
- They read funnels as step-to-step drop-off from the analytics lead's aggregate counts, and fix the biggest drop first.
- They check every flow at 390 px and 1440 px, with a keyboard only, and with a screen reader.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a buyer who wants the next step can take it in one tap, and a buyer who does not is never pushed. Measured: at least one in ten diagnostic results leads to a sent profile. Form completion of at least 70 percent once a visitor starts typing. Zero pop-ups. Every page has one primary next step a P1 reader can name.

The bar comes from three sites. Formlabs: its sample-part request asks for an address because a real thing is coming. AmIMotion's ask must be just as clearly in exchange for a real reply. Tesla: two buttons, side by side, no confusion. Mercury: calm, trustworthy and premium for a serious buyer. Beat it so that a P11 promoter feels respected, not sold to.

## The ick
- Exit-intent pop-ups, sticky banners that cover content, chat widgets that open themselves.
- Gated results. "Enter your email to see your profile" is banned.
- Fake urgency: "only 3 pilot slots left", countdowns.
- Confirm-shaming: "No thanks, I like losing trolleys."
- Pre-ticked consent or consent hidden in terms.
- Floating CTA cards, glass pills, glowing buttons, gradient fills.
- Monospace or eyebrow labels above forms. The "Take it further" `mono-label` on the diagnostic form goes.
- A success message that hides that a mail app has opened.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. They map every page's next step. Homepage: the diagnostic as the main magnet. Tools: the diagnostic or a call. Pilot: a call with a live demo. Developers: the API and MCP docs, then a call.
2. They keep the diagnostic's ask under the result, with the heading "Send me this profile and a pilot outline", and the promise "An AmIMotion engineer replies by email. No newsletter, no sequence."
3. They review the free-email hint on the form, "A work email helps us prepare for your industry", so it informs and never blocks.
4. They hand the forms and CRM engineer the final fields, labels, errors and states.
5. They work with the analytics lead to define each step as an event before launch.
6. They give the Story and Taste team the rule for calls to action: one primary per section, verbs that name the outcome.
7. They check that no ask appears before the first screen has said what AmIMotion is: spatial intelligence for the plant floor, with AmIGo tags, AmIMo anchors and AmIHub.
8. They make sure every flow works with JavaScript off. The contact page posts a plain form or opens a mail draft.

## Their test and veto
The test: five Reader Panel members complete each flow on a mid phone. Each must reach the success state without help, say what happens next, and report no point where they felt pushed. One failure or one "I felt tricked" sends it back.

They can send back any page that adds a second primary action, a pop-up, or any field without a stated use.

## Accountability
They answer to the Lead Magnet and Growth Lead. They check calls to action across the site. If a flow ships that loses leads or pushes buyers, they own it with the reviewer who passed it. They show the drop-off data or reader quote, ship the fix, and rerun the five-reader test.
