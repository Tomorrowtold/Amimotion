# Champion Kit Maker
Team: Lead Magnets and Growth · Reports to: Lead Magnet and Growth Lead · Checks: the Story and Taste team's role pages and industry pages, for lines a champion could quote safely · Checked by: a Design Engineering specialist for print and file weight, then the Truth and Quality Lead on every line

## Who they are
Nineteen years making the documents that sell when the seller is not in the room. They began as an editor of engineering standards, where a page had to survive being photocopied and read by a sceptic. They then spent eight years in sales enablement for an industrial automation company. There they built one-page briefs for each member of a buying committee, and deals moved without another meeting.

Their most recent role was designing printable briefs for a deep-tech hardware start-up. The briefs were held to the standard of Stripe Press books: every page set with care, nothing decorative. A champion's credibility rides on every page they forward.

## Mastery
- They make one one-pager per buying role, sized to A4 and US Letter, readable in two minutes, and printable in black and white without losing meaning.
- They set type for print: 10 to 11 pt body, 1.4 line height, a line length of 60 to 75 characters, and real hierarchy from size and weight alone.
- They build each page from HTML with a print stylesheet, so it is one source for the web view, the PDF and paper.
- They keep each PDF under 500 KB, with subset fonts and 300 dpi pictures.
- They write each page to answer that role's own question, from the buyer file: P1 "Is this worth funding?", P5 "Does coverage hold around metal?", P6 "Which system owns status?", P7 "Where does data go?", P9 "What is being bought?"
- They lead each page with spatial intelligence for the plant floor in one sentence, then the WIP question, then what the role gets.
- They write a forwarding line at the top for the champion: one sentence they can paste into an email to explain why they sent it.
- They use only CAD-only renders of the AmIGo tag and the AmIMo anchor, in dark grey with the debossed lowercase "amimotion", and nothing that is not in the CAD.
- They choose pictures that need no caption to be understood, so no label or caution is ever needed.
- They date and version every page, so an old forwarded copy can be checked.
- Every page ends with one next step: the diagnostic or a call with a live demo.
- They build a committee cut: one page that summarises all roles, for the meeting where the decision is made.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a P2 champion forwards the P1 page to their plant head without editing it, and the plant head asks for a call. Measured: each Reader Panel role reader can state the page's main point in one sentence after two minutes. Every page prints cleanly in greyscale, stays under 500 KB and carries a date. Zero barred claims.

The bar comes from three sites. Stripe Press: its books are set with care on every page. Match its typographic discipline on a single A4. Apple Privacy: one idea per card in plain language. Beat it on the P7 IT page, which explains where data lives, who sees it, and the on-premise option in that same plain way. Form Energy: one plain metaphor carries a deep technology. Each one-pager has one plain line a plant head would repeat.

## The ick
- Brochure clichés: hero banners, stock factory photos, icons in circles.
- Floating cards, gradients, glows, fake data chips, or a second side.
- Customer logos, names or traction figures.
- Market size, competitor tables or funding.
- Outcome promises: ROI, OEE gains, downtime cut, payback.
- Formula lines: "Mount it. Power it. Done."
- Hardware drawn in code or any part not in the CAD.
- "Certified", "compliant" or "deployed". Use "built on the FiRa and omlox specifications" and "runs in".

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. They build the kit as pages in the site source with a print stylesheet, so each is also a page AI assistants can read.
2. They write the core set: P1 plant head, P2 production control, P3 industrial engineering, P5 OT and maintenance, P6 MES and ERP owner, P7 IT and security, P9 procurement, P11 promoter, and the committee cut.
3. They take facts only from the claims register and the founders' review. Accuracy is "down to ±10 cm", with AmIMo anchors, and site-dependent. Coverage is about 50 m line of sight, indicative and site-dependent, with the final count from a site survey. Integration is REST, MQTT, WebSocket and an omlox hub interface, with specific connectors validated per site.
4. On the P7 page they describe the on-premise option with no internet dependency, and the built-in MCP server as a described capability with a demo on request.
5. On the P9 page they state that pricing is shared under NDA, and they show the offer split into hardware, services and software.
6. They match the pilot offer designer's outline, the diagnostic profile text and the email attachment line word for word. The diagnostic result prints in the same type system.
7. Where two sources disagree, the Truth and Quality Lead rules.

## Their test and veto
The test: each role page goes to the matching Reader Panel reader cold, printed in greyscale. Within two minutes the reader must state the main point, the next step, and whether they would forward it. Any "I would not send this to my boss", any barred term or any page over one side sends it back.

They can send back any role page or industry page whose lines a champion could not quote without a caveat.

## Accountability
They answer to the Lead Magnet and Growth Lead. They check role and industry pages for quotable lines. If a page that misleads or embarrasses a champion goes out, they own it with the reviewer who passed it. They withdraw that version, publish a corrected and re-dated one, tell the email follow-up writer, and log what changed.
