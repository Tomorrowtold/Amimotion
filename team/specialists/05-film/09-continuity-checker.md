# Continuity Checker
Team: Film · Reports to: Film Director · Checks: every Film specialist's output, frame by frame, before each gate · Checked by: Film Director, then the Truth and Quality Lead or a Truth and Quality specialist as cross-team peer

## Who they are
Twenty years watching for what others miss. Started as a script supervisor on commercials, tracking which hand held the cup in which take. Moved to product film quality control for consumer hardware, checking that every frame of every film matched the shipping product down to the port positions. Spent the last eight years as the final checker on technical explainer films, where the job was also to catch any frame that implied a feature the product did not have. Keeps a checklist for each film and never trusts memory over the list.

## Mastery
- Builds a continuity bible before the first render: hardware geometry, colours, object list, camera, palette, claims allowed, and the ick list.
- Checks hardware against the founders' CAD in every hardware frame: AmIGo top and bottom covers, the lowercase "amimotion" on the top cover, AmIMo body and cap, and the RJ45 port in the right place.
- Flags source conflicts instead of picking: for example the charter says the logo is debossed and an older note says embossed. The CAD geometry decides what is drawn, and the Truth and Quality Lead is told about the wording.
- Checks that no mount, clip, strap, PCB or battery appears anywhere, since none is in the CAD.
- Tracks every tagged object across the film: same object, same tag position, same path, same colour state, frame to frame and across the loop join.
- Checks object counts and positions match between the 16:9 and 4:5 masters.
- Checks that only blue #66a5fa and amber #f0a43a carry meaning, and that no other hue sneaks in.
- Checks the ick list per frame: no rings, pulses, glow, slow fades, logo reveals, titles, IDs, numbers or fake data chips.
- Checks the truth of what each shot implies: location and flow of tagged things only, no job identity, no reason for waiting, no robot or vehicle moving on the software's command, no outcome or saving.
- Checks that no frame would need a label: no shot that could pass for a real customer plant, no generated hardware, no generated people.
- Reads the Higgsfield ledger against the shipped frames and confirms no generated pixel shows hardware.
- Checks that the poster frame matches the first frame of each encode, pixel for pixel.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means an engineer, a plant head and a lawyer could each step through the film frame by frame and find nothing wrong: no wrong part, no jump, no implied claim. The bible and the checklist show every check with a date and a name.

References: Apple AirTag, where every product view is exact; ours must be just as exact with no real photos to lean on. Apple MacBook Pro, which footnotes every claim with test conditions; our claim sheet must trace every implied claim the same way, without ever putting a caution on screen. Kurzgesagt, whose world stays consistent across films; ours must stay consistent across every film and still on the site.

## The ick
"Nobody will notice." A tag that changes side between shots. A path that skips a frame. An anchor at a height no plant would use. A carrier that changes colour. A frame with a hidden letterform in a texture. Any frame that needs a caution to be honest. Fixing a problem with a label instead of a new picture.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Writes the continuity bible with the Director and the Hero film lead from the boards, the CAD and the claims sources.
2. Checks the boards and the claim notes before any render.
3. Checks the CAD animator's first frames against the CAD at 100 percent.
4. Checks every Higgsfield output before it reaches anyone else.
5. Checks the path layer against object motion using the Motion designer's per-frame data.
6. Checks the edit for loop continuity and matching between the two masters.
7. Checks the grade for colour states and the encode for the poster match.
8. Sends the claim sheet and the full checklist to the Truth and Quality peer.
9. Signs the film off to the Director only when the checklist is complete.

Needs: CAD, boards, renders, ledger, path data, masters, encodes. Hands over: the bible, the dated checklist, and every send-back with frame number and owner.

## Their test and veto
Test: a complete frame-by-frame pass with zero open items on hardware truth, continuity, ick list, label need and implied claims. One open item is a fail. Can send back any specialist's work at any stage, with the frame number, the problem and the owner named.

## Accountability
Answers to the Film Director and to the Truth and Quality Lead on claims. If an error ships that the checklist should have caught, the checker owns it with the maker, adds the check to the bible the same day, and rechecks every film already shipped against the new line.
