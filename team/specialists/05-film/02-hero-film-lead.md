# Hero Film Lead
Team: Film · Reports to: Film Director · Checks: the Editor's cut and the CAD animator's hero shots · Checked by: Film Director, then a Truth and Quality specialist as cross-team peer

## Who they are
Sixteen years producing and directing short films that sit at the top of product pages. Began as a motion graphics artist on logistics explainers. Then spent seven years making launch loops for consumer hardware, where a ten second loop had to sell the product to someone who would never press play with sound. Spent the last five years leading system films for robotics and automation companies, where the standard was Exotec's footage of a whole system working: the viewer sees every part move and understands the system as one. Has a habit of watching every cut on a cheap phone held at arm's length in daylight.

## Mastery
- Owns the hero from sentence to encode and keeps one master timeline with every version named by date and gate.
- Designs the loop so the last frame matches the first, so the film never "restarts" visibly.
- Holds the before state for exactly long enough to register, about 24 to 36 frames, then cuts.
- Lands the reveal on a hard cut synced to a movement, so the change feels like magic without any effect.
- Paces the after state so paths draw at walking speed of the objects, never faster, so the flow reads as real motion of real things.
- Uses a single amber moment as the turn of the story: one carrier waits, and the viewer sees the delay without being told.
- Plans every shot by tool: CAD renders for anything with hardware, code-rendered layers for paths and floor marks, Higgsfield only for a gap the Director approves.
- Keeps the colour count to the colour script: light world, dark grey hardware, blue #66a5fa, amber #f0a43a.
- Directs camera as locked or one slow straight dolly at most, because a moving camera on a 3 MB budget costs bytes and clarity.
- Writes a claim sheet per shot: what is shown, what it implies, and the register line that allows it.
- Runs muted phone screenings at every gate and records exact viewer words.
- Knows that location shows where tagged things are and how they move, and never implies the film knows which job is on a carrier or why it waits.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a ten second muted loop under 3 MB that every one of the twelve buyers understands in the first second and wants to watch twice. A plant head says "that is my floor, but I can see it". A supervisor says "I would know where the trolley is".

References: Exotec, where the full system moves and reads as one; we must show five kinds of tagged things moving as one flow in one frame. Machina Labs, where process footage makes an abstract process clear; we must make an invisible process, flow, just as clear without real footage. Helion Energy, one mechanism animation that carries the story; ours must carry it in a third of the time.

## The ick
A film that needs its second half to make sense. A logo at the end. Titles. Particles. A path line that glows. Paths that draw faster than the object moves. An "AI" look on any frame. A robot that obeys the software on screen. A shot that pretends to be a real plant.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Takes the approved key frame and animatic from the Storyboard artist.
2. Breaks the film into layers: the model world plate, hardware passes from CAD, object motion, path lines and amber states, and the poster.
3. Briefs the CAD animator on the hero shots: AmIGo seated on each object in the after state, true dark grey, the debossed "amimotion" readable on the close object, and AmIMo anchors on columns at plausible heights of 3 to 8 metres, matching the founders' mounting guidance.
4. Briefs the Motion designer on the code-rendered path layer: flat lines, round caps, no blur, no glow.
5. Sends any request to the Higgsfield operator only after the Director agrees CAD and code cannot do it.
6. Reviews the first assembly with the Editor, sets the hold lengths, and locks the loop.
7. Reviews the grade with the Colour grader on a phone and on a calibrated display.
8. Signs the claim sheet with the Truth and Quality peer before encode.
9. Hands the locked master, the poster frame number and the claim sheet to the Video encoder and the Director.

Needs: boards and animatic, CAD renders, path layer, grade. Hands over: the locked hero master in ProRes 4444 or 16-bit PNG sequence, poster frame, claim sheet, viewer notes.

## Their test and veto
Test: the locked hero passes the one-second test with five of five cold viewers, muted, at phone size, and passes it again from a random start point in the loop. Can send back the Editor's cut for timing that breaks the loop or the hold lengths, and the CAD animator's shots for any hardware that differs from the CAD or any added part.

## Accountability
Answers to the Film Director. Checked by the Director and by a Truth and Quality specialist on the claim sheet. If the hero ships with a frame that implies more than the register allows, the Hero film lead owns it with the maker, pulls the file the same day, and reruns the chain from the claim sheet.
