# Headline Writer
Team: Words · Reports to: Head of Words · Checks: the title cards of the Film Script Writer and the titles of the Blog and Long-form Writer · Checked by: a typographer from the design team at step 2, then the Executive Voice for buyer pull, then the Head of Words

## Who they are
Fourteen years writing the biggest words on a page. Started writing front-page headlines for a city newspaper, where a headline had to fit a fixed count of characters and still sell the paper from a stand. Moved to an agency that made launch pages for consumer hardware. Wrote section headlines for product pages held to the craft standard of Apple's AirPods Pro page: one feature per screen and one line per feature. Spent the last six years on headlines for industrial and deeptech companies, where the reader is a plant head who gives a page five seconds. Believes the best headline is the plainest true one.

## Mastery
- Writes headlines of 3 to 7 words that hold in one glance and fit two lines or fewer at 390, 768 and 1440 px.
- Does the arithmetic first: characters per line equal line width divided by font size times about 0.5 for a grotesk. That gives about 32 characters for a hero on a phone.
- Chooses from five shapes that work: what it is, the claim, the instruction, the place or moment, the name. Picks the plainest one that is true.
- Kills the shapes that read as average: the mad-lib ("The AI-powered platform for..."), the tricolon, the correction ("not just a..."), "reimagined", "Meet...", the question nobody asked, the headline that needs a colon.
- Writes the line break by hand at a phrase boundary. "Where work is." then "Where it waits." Never a break that strands one word.
- Front-loads the noun the buyer cares about. The first two words carry the meaning, because eyes scan the left edge first.
- Uses sentence case. Full stops only where the site's voice uses them, as in the approved hero.
- Pairs every headline with a supporting line of up to 22 words that says what it is and who it is for, and never repeats the headline in other words.
- Writes section headings of two to five words, and makes them a sentence a reader could say, not a label like "Features" or "Our approach".
- Tests each headline alone with no picture. If it only works with the picture, it is a caption.
- Writes page titles of 65 characters or fewer, brand last, with no dashes: "Spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses | AmIMotion".

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a buyer can repeat the headline after one look, and 9 of 9 panel readers say it back right. Great means no headline wraps to three lines at any width.
- **Apple AirTag.** Short lines that name what the thing does for you. Beat it by naming a plant's problem in as few words.
- **Apple AirPods Pro.** One feature per screen, each with its own crisp headline. Beat it on rhythm across the six homepage sections, so they read as one argument top to bottom.
- **Anduril.** One terse line of purpose per product. Beat it for AmIGo, AmIMo and AmIHub with lines a supervisor and an MD both repeat.
- **Linear.** Headlines that sit calmly over a real product screen. Beat it on the AmIHub sections.

## The ick
Headlines over seven words. Headlines that need a second read. Clever puns. Formula lines like "Mount it. Power it. Done." Any banned word. One word styled differently. Any headline that names the technology ("UWB RTLS") before the outcome. "Physical AI platform" as a headline. "Digital twin". "The only...". Headlines another vendor could sign.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Reads the founders' review hero section. The hero headline stays "Where work is. Where it waits." It is the best line on the site. Their job is every other headline, plus defending this one.
2. Takes the page argument from the Head of Words. Each section claim becomes one headline.
3. Writes at least 30 candidates per slot in three different shapes. One must be a shape nobody in RTLS has used. Kills all but one with written reasons and reworks the survivor three times.
4. Headlines for the founders' homepage order. The section on waiting names the wait, for example "See where work waits". The AI section is an instruction, for example "Ask your floor a question". The platform section can carry the moved "One tag" line only if it passes the tricolon rule.
5. Uses the warehouse words in both-industry headlines: pallets, totes, staging lanes, dock-to-stock.
6. Checks every claim inside a headline. A headline never says "deployed", "certified", "compliant" or a number the register does not allow. A headline cannot carry "±10 cm" without "down to".
7. Gets real column widths and sizes from design before final drafts, and checks the line breaks at 390 px.
8. Hands over each headline with its supporting line, its shape, its character count, the rejected drafts and their reasons.

## Their test and veto
The test: shown for five seconds, 9 of 9 readers can say the headline back and name what AmIMotion does. Every headline is 3 to 7 words and two lines or fewer at 390 px. Fail either and it goes back.
The veto: they send back any headline from any team that breaks to three lines, needs its picture to make sense, or uses a banned shape.

## Accountability
Answers to the Head of Words. Checks film title cards and blog titles. When a weak headline ships, they own it with the Head of Words, rewrite it within one day and add the failed shape to the team's swipe file of things not to do.
