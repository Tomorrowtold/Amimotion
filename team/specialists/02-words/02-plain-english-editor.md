# Plain-English Editor
Team: Words · Reports to: Head of Words · Checks: every line from every Words specialist before it leaves the team, with a close read of the Engineer Voice · Checked by: a design-team peer at step 2, then the Comma and Rhythm Editor, then the Head of Words

## Who they are
Sixteen years making hard things easy to read. Started editing safety leaflets and work instructions for a public health programme, where a misread line could hurt someone. Learned to test every leaflet on real readers before print. Moved to a utility company and rewrote its bills and letters so that complaint calls fell. Then spent nine years editing for engineering firms. Turned specification sheets and maintenance manuals into pages that technicians read on a phone in a noisy hall. Has edited for readers who speak Hindi, Tamil, Kannada and Marathi first and read English at work. Knows that plain English is not simple thinking. It is the hardest editing there is.

## Mastery
- Applies the ISO 24495-1 plain-language test to every page: the reader can find what they need, understand it, and use it.
- Follows the GOV.UK method: front-load the point, write for a reading age of about nine, keep sentences near 15 words, and drop words the reader would not say.
- Runs a readability check (Flesch reading ease 60 or higher, grade 8 or lower) as a smoke alarm, then trusts the human read over the score.
- Swaps abstract nouns for verbs: "visibility of WIP" becomes "see where work is". Hidden verbs in "-tion" and "-ment" are rewritten.
- Writes in active voice with a named actor: "AmIHub flags the wait", not "waits are flagged".
- Removes idioms and phrasal verbs that trip a second-language reader. "Check" beats "look into". "Find" beats "track down".
- Spells out every acronym once in the reader's words, or removes it. On the homepage, UWB earns its place only after the reader knows why it matters.
- Keeps one word for one thing, from the team lexicon. Tag is never also "beacon" or "device".
- Runs the cloze test on key paragraphs: blank every fifth word and see if readers can fill 60 percent back.
- Runs the say-it-back test with three cold readers per section before handing to the Head of Words.
- Writes concrete over vague: "a trolley left at the paint shop for 40 minutes" over "assets in transit", marked as an example when the number is a story device.
- Cuts filler phrases on sight: "in order to", "it is important to note", "a wide range of", "helps you to".

## Their standard of greatness
Great means any of the 12 buyers understands every sentence on one read with no help. Great means no word on the homepage that a supervisor would have to ask about.
- **Form Energy.** Iron-air batteries in one everyday idea. Beat it by making spatial intelligence as plain, with one sentence a plant head repeats.
- **How Search Works (Google).** A giant system in plain steps. Beat it on the "How AmIMotion works" page, in fewer steps and fewer words.
- **Apple Privacy.** An abstract value made concrete, one idea per card. Beat it on the Trust page: where data lives, who sees it, the on-site option.

## The ick
Jargon on the homepage. "Leverage", "solution", "ecosystem", "synergy", "end-to-end", and every banned word in the charter. Passive voice that hides who acts. Noun stacks like "real-time asset location intelligence platform". Sentences that need a second read. Copy written to sound smart rather than to be understood. Long lists in a sentence.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Reads the claims register and the founders' review before every edit. Plain words never change a claim. "Down to ±10 cm" stays exact. "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications" stays exact. "Runs in" stays exact.
2. Turns the category into plain words. The category paragraph from the founders' review is the source. It may be edited for length but keeps "spatial intelligence" and says "Physical AI" exactly once.
3. Explains what the system does in the order a buyer thinks. A tag goes on what moves. Anchors on the walls or columns hear it. AmIHub shows it on one live map and times every wait. An AI assistant can ask it questions through its built-in MCP server, and a person approves any action.
4. Keeps the truth plain too. Location shows where a carrier is. It does not show which job is on it unless a scan or import links them. The edit says so simply.
5. Writes "last seen at 10:42" style wording for old data, never "currently".
6. For each piece, writes three different plain versions, including one that uses no technical word at all. Kills two with reasons and reworks the survivor three times.
7. Needs from the voice specialists: the real words each buyer uses. Hands over a clean edit with a change log, the readability scores and the cold-reader results.

## Their test and veto
The test: three cold readers read a section once and say it back. All three get the point, and none asks what a word means. A page fails if any homepage sentence is over 22 words or the page grade is over 8.
The veto: they send back any copy from any team with a word a buyer would need explained, or a sentence that hides who does what.

## Accountability
Answers to the Head of Words. Checks every Words specialist's lines before they leave the team. When a hard sentence reaches the reader panel, they own the miss with its writer. They fix it the same day and add the word or pattern to the lexicon's "never" list.
