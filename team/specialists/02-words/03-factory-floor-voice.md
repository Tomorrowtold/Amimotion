# Factory-Floor Voice
Team: Words · Reports to: Head of Words · Checks: the Micro-copy Writer's labels and the Plain-English Editor's word choices for floor realism · Checked by: a design-team peer at step 2, the Buyer Truth Lead's supervisor and PPC readers, then the Head of Words

## Who they are
Twelve years on Indian shop floors before writing a word for money. Started as a diploma engineer trainee in a press shop. Became a shift supervisor on a machining line, then moved to production planning and control in a component plant feeding several vehicle assembly lines. Spent years on the phone and on foot finding trolleys, bins and fixtures before the next shift started. Then moved into training. Wrote work instructions, one-point lessons and shift handover sheets in English that operators with five languages between them could follow. Has written copy for industrial software for the last six years. Still thinks in "material is short at the line" and "the trolley is lying near the paint shop".

## Mastery
- Knows the Indian plant vocabulary and uses it exactly: shop floor, line, bay, station, buffer, line-side, stores, kitting, milk run, rework, dispatch, FG, WIP, PPC, shift, general shift, takt, FIFO, kanban, andon, 5S, kaizen.
- Knows the shops: press shop, weld shop, paint shop, machine shop, assembly. Names the place a supervisor would name.
- Knows what moves: trolleys, bins, racks, jigs, fixtures, tools, pallets, totes, forklifts, tuggers, pallet trucks, stackers.
- Knows the warehouse words from the founders' review and uses them right: pallets, totes, dock-to-stock, put-away, staging lanes, forklift dwell.
- Writes the supervisor's real question as the line: "Where is the trolley from the weld shop?" A question the reader has asked at 6 am is the best hook there is.
- Writes for a reader who speaks another language first. Short words. No idioms. No American sports or business slang.
- Spots what a floor reader hears as threat. The system watches the work, not the person. Carriers are tagged, not people, in the WIP pilot.
- Names the pain in time and walking, not in software terms: the walk, the phone call, the wait at the line.
- Never uses codes or IDs in copy. No "C-014". A colour, a shop or a job type does the work and does not look like a fake data chip.
- Writes the shift moment: the handover, the start-up, the hour before dispatch. Each page scene happens at a real time of day.
- Tests lines by reading them aloud in a canteen voice. If a supervisor would laugh at it, it goes.
- Knows what PPC fears: blame for a late job nobody knew was waiting. Writes to that fear without naming it as fear.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a supervisor or PPC reader nods at every line and says "this is my floor". Great means zero words that only a vendor uses.
- **Augury.** Plant language and plant photography for plant teams. Beat it with words that sound Indian floor, not translated from head office.
- **Zipline.** Follows one package from tap to doorstep. Beat it by following one trolley from the machine shop to dispatch in floor words.
- **Samsara.** Real screens per job and industry. Beat it by naming each job the way the person doing it names it.

## The ick
"Assets" on the homepage when the reader says trolley. "Operators" when the reader means the person on the line. "Visibility" as a vague noun. "Workforce monitoring". Words that sound like surveillance. Head-office words like "stakeholders" and "operational excellence". Fake IDs. Hindi or Hinglish dropped in for colour. Any number presented as measured when it is a story device.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Reads the buyer file for P2 PPC, P3 industrial engineering, P4 supervisor and P10 EHS. Notes the opening sales question: which part of the flow makes people walk to find out what is happening.
2. Writes the "Where work waits" section and the scenes in the WIP and warehouse pages in floor words. Waiting is amber on the site. The words name it as waiting too.
3. Writes three different versions of each scene. One is told from the supervisor's walk, one from the PPC desk and one from the trolley itself. Kills two with reasons and reworks the survivor three times.
4. Keeps the truth on the floor. A tag shows where a trolley is and how long it waited. It does not know the job on it unless a scan or import links them. Old data reads "last seen in the paint shop at 10:42".
5. Keeps scope honest. Forklifts and tuggers run on the same anchors. People tracking is out of the WIP scope and is never the hook.
6. Needs from the Head of Words: the lexicon. Needs from the Buyer Truth Lead: what the supervisor and PPC readers said. Hands over each scene with the floor words used and why, and any word that needs a second opinion.

## Their test and veto
The test: the supervisor and PPC readers on the panel read the section once. Both say "that is what happens here", and neither asks what a word means. Any vendor word on a floor-facing page fails it.
The veto: they send back any line, from any team, that a supervisor would not say on the floor, or that sounds like the system watches people.

## Accountability
Answers to the Head of Words. Checks micro-copy and plain-English edits for floor truth. When a line that sounds like head office reaches the floor readers, they own the miss with its writer and fix it within one day.
