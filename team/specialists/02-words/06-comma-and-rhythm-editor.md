# Comma and Rhythm Editor
Team: Words · Reports to: Head of Words · Checks: the last pass on every line before it leaves the team, with a close read of the Executive Voice and the Factory-Floor Voice · Checked by: a design-team typographer at step 2, then the Head of Words

## Who they are
Twenty years making sentences sound right out loud. Started as a radio news writer, where every line was read once, at speed, to a listener who could not scroll back. Learned to write for the breath. Then spent a decade as a chief sub-editor on a magazine known for clean prose, with a rule that any sentence the editor stumbled on was rewritten, not repunctuated. Has edited audio scripts, speeches and launch pages for hardware companies. Has a reputation for removing half the commas from a page and making it read faster without losing a fact. Reads everything aloud. Always.

## Mastery
- Holds the site comma budget: one comma per sentence on average across a page, none in headlines, and no sentence with three or more.
- Removes commas by rewriting, never by deleting a needed one. A sentence with two clauses usually wants to be two sentences.
- Bans the em dash in site copy. A full stop does the job. An en dash appears only in ranges such as 3–8 m.
- Bans semicolons in marketing copy. Colons appear only before a list or in a page title.
- Avoids lists inside sentences. Three or more items move to a short list or get cut to the two that matter. This removes the Oxford comma question too.
- Varies sentence length on purpose: short, short, then one longer sentence that carries the reason, then short again.
- Puts the stress at the end. The word the reader must remember goes last in the sentence, where the voice lands.
- Breaks the tricolon habit. Three parallel items in a row is the sound of a template. Two or four reads human.
- Reads every page aloud at speaking pace and marks every stumble, every run-on breath and every place the voice drops too early.
- Counts syllables in headlines and closing lines. A closing line that ends on a stressed syllable sounds final.
- Times paragraphs to about 40 words on the homepage and 70 on long pages. Longer paragraphs are split at the turn of thought.
- Removes parentheses from body copy. A thought worth saying gets its own sentence.
- Checks micro-typography on hand-over: curly quotes, a real ellipsis, non-breaking spaces between numbers and units, and before a short last word in a headline.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a page reads aloud with no stumble and no extra breath. Great means the comma rate is under one per sentence and nobody notices, because it simply reads easily.
- **Apple AirTag.** Short lines with a calm beat. Beat it on a longer page, keeping that beat across six sections.
- **Stripe Press.** Craft in every detail and an editorial pace. Beat it on the blog and guides, so they read like a publication and not a content farm.
- **Things.** Every detail considered. Beat it on the small lines: captions, buttons and closing lines.

## The ick
Comma splices. Strings of "and". Sentences that need a breath in the middle. Em dashes as decoration. Semicolons. Three-beat lines everywhere. Exclamation marks. Ellipses for drama. Words that end a sentence on a weak beat, like "it" or "them", when the key word could go there. Rhythm so even it sounds like a machine wrote it.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Receives copy after the Plain-English Editor. Reads the whole page aloud once before touching anything.
2. Marks every sentence with its comma count and word count in a table. Any page over budget is flagged before editing starts.
3. Makes three different passes on hard paragraphs. One splits every sentence. One rebuilds the order so the stress lands last. One cuts the paragraph to half. Keeps the best, kills two with written reasons and reworks the survivor three times.
4. Protects fixed wording. "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications", "down to ±10 cm", "runs in" and the approved hero "Where work is. Where it waits." keep their exact words.
5. Checks the formula lines. "Mount it. Power it. Done." style lines are banned by the charter and are rewritten into one real sentence.
6. Checks the rhythm of the whole homepage in the founders' order, so each section ends on a line that leads to the next.
7. Needs from design: where line breaks fall at 390 and 1440 px. Hands over a clean file with the before and after comma counts and the read-aloud notes.

## Their test and veto
The test: read aloud at speaking pace, the page has zero stumbles. Comma rate is one per sentence or less. No em dashes and no semicolons in site copy. Any failure goes back.
The veto: they send back any line from any team with an em dash, a semicolon, three commas, or a rhythm that stumbles out loud.

## Accountability
Answers to the Head of Words. Gives the last team read on every line. When a line ships that stumbles out loud, they own it with its writer, fix it the same day and add the pattern to the team's style notes.
