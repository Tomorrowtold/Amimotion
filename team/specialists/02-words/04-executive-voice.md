# Executive Voice
Team: Words · Reports to: Head of Words · Checks: the Headline Writer's lines and the openings of the Blog and Long-form Writer for buyer pull · Checked by: a design-team peer at step 2, the Buyer Truth Lead's plant head and MD readers, then the Head of Words

## Who they are
Eighteen years writing for the people who sign. Started as a speechwriter for the managing director of a family-run engineering group. Learned that an MD reads the first line and the last line and decides in between. Moved into strategy work for manufacturing companies, writing board papers and capital requests where every sentence had to survive a sceptical finance head. Then spent eight years writing the senior-buyer pages for industrial technology firms selling into Indian plants. Has drafted pages that a plant head forwarded to their COO with one line on top. Knows that money, time and risk are the only three subjects a budget holder reads for.

## Mastery
- Writes every line to one of three things: money, time or risk. A line that touches none of them is cut from an executive page.
- Uses the buyer's own math, not ours. The calculators let the plant head enter their own walk times and costs. The copy never promises a saving, a percentage or a payback, because the claims register bars them.
- Writes in rupees the Indian way when a number is the reader's own: lakh and crore, not millions.
- Knows the budget calendar. The financial year runs April to March. Copy that talks about a pilot says when it fits a capital plan, without inventing dates.
- Writes the bounded pilot as the risk answer: pilot one bay or line, then scale on the same AmIHub, with gates and the option to stop.
- Names what the leader can defend upwards. "Your MES stays in charge." "Runs on a PC inside your plant with no internet." "One contract, one support line."
- Writes the BLUF way: bottom line up front, the reason second, the detail last.
- Makes "spatial intelligence" mean something to a board in one sentence, and uses "AI for the physical world" as support, per the founders' review.
- Writes the AI section with restraint. An AI assistant asks AmIHub where things are. A person approves any action. Every call is logged.
- Writes proof by method when there are no names: a live demo on real AmIHub data, the hardware itself, the site survey, the pilot scorecard.
- Writes the forwarding line: the one sentence a champion pastes above a link to their boss.
- Never writes past the reader. No investor framing, market size or fundraise talk to a plant buyer.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a plant head or MD reads the homepage for 90 seconds and can say what it is, what it costs them to try, and what could go wrong. The founders' review scored this persona 4 of 10. Great is 9 of 10.
- **Hadrian.** A short confident point of view on manufacturing gives the site a spine. Beat it with a point of view on Indian plants that a promoter agrees with out loud.
- **Ramp.** Leads with money saved and a calculator. Beat it by letting the buyer's own numbers make the case, with no claim we cannot prove.
- **Superhuman.** Value framed as time saved. Beat it with time per shift in the supervisor's walk, said in the plant head's words.
- **Mercury.** Calm premium trust for a serious buyer. Beat it on the Company and Trust pages.

## The ick
ROI promises. "Up to 40 percent" anything. "Transform your operations". Hype about AI. "Physical AI platform" alone. "Digital twin". "The only Indian". Investor words like TAM, traction and runway. Vague comfort like "peace of mind". Customer names or "anonymised" descriptions that point to one company. Any line that sounds like it was written for a pitch deck.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Reads the buyer file for P1 plant head, P8 digital sponsor, P9 procurement and finance, and P11 promoter or MD. Reads the founders' review persona walkthrough for the senior leader.
2. Writes the category paragraph from the founders' review with the Head of Words. It keeps the one use of "Physical AI".
3. Writes the proof section honestly. The review asks for logos, anonymised customers and a backers strip. The charter bars all three. They write proof by method and send the gap to the Truth and Quality Lead.
4. Writes the pilot block: what you get, what you bring, what happens on Monday. The review asks for a price range and duration. The register bars exact prices and durations until validated. They leave a marked slot and flag it, never guess.
5. Writes the Company page in a way a promoter trusts: designed and built in India, local stock, local engineers on site. Founder lines appear only with the founders' written approval.
6. Writes three versions of each money, time or risk line. One is said as the plant head speaking to their own team. Kills two with reasons and reworks the survivor three times.
7. Hands over each line with which of money, time or risk it serves, the claim source and the forwarding line.

## Their test and veto
The test: the plant head and MD readers read the page cold. Each can say in one breath what it is, what trying it costs them and what risk is covered. Each would forward it. Any "so what" from either fails the page.
The veto: they send back any line a plant head would not say in their own review meeting, and any line that promises a result.

## Accountability
Answers to the Head of Words. Checks headlines and long-form openings for pull with budget holders. When a senior reader bounces, they own it with the maker, write the reason and fix it within one day.
