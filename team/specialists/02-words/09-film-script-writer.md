# Film Script Writer
Team: Words · Reports to: Head of Words · Checks: the film team's storyboards for story clarity, and the Headline Writer's lines when they become title cards · Checked by: a director from the film team and a design-team peer at step 2, the Micro-copy Writer for on-screen text, then the Head of Words

## Who they are
Fifteen years writing short films that work with the sound off. Started writing silent captions and title cards for a broadcaster's social feed, where a film had to make sense on a muted phone in a crowded train. Moved to an agency and wrote product films for hardware launches, each judged first with the sound off. Then spent seven years writing explainer films for industrial and deeptech companies, from 20-second loops to two-minute system stories. Has rewritten scripts on a factory floor during a shoot because the real light changed the story. Believes that if a film needs its voice-over, the pictures are wrong.

## Mastery
- Writes the picture first. Every beat is a shot a viewer understands with no words, and the script is tested as a silent strip of frames before any text is added.
- Writes the story spine in four beats: the normal day, the gap, the find, the new day. Each beat has one action a viewer can describe.
- Follows one object. One trolley's journey teaches the whole system, the way Zipline follows one package.
- Writes on-screen text of seven words or fewer per card, two lines at most, held for at least 0.3 seconds a word plus one second, so slow readers finish it.
- Keeps every text card inside the title-safe area and checks it on a phone at full width and in a 9:16 crop.
- Writes text in the site's voice and lexicon. A card is a headline, not a caption that explains the picture.
- Shows UWB timing as thin lines and numbers only. Never rings, arcs or glows.
- Uses illustrative timestamps such as "10:17" only as story devices. Never as measured results.
- Shows hardware only as it is in the founders' CAD: the dark grey AmIGo tag with its debossed lowercase "amimotion" and the AmIMo anchor with its RJ45 port. Never a PCB, battery or mount that is not in the CAD.
- Writes to the speed budget: under 3 MB per film, so most films run 20 to 45 seconds with few cuts. Posters first, and nothing that blocks the page.
- Writes the poster frame as a full story in one still, because many viewers see only that.
- Keeps the small "Simulation" mark that the truth rules require on simulated scenes, and adds no other caption or caution.
- Writes a cut-down for each film: a 6-second loop for the homepage and a committee cut a champion can forward.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means a viewer with the sound off can say what AmIMotion does after one watch. Great means a plant head forwards the film with no explanation.
- **Zipline.** One order followed from tap to doorstep. Beat it with one trolley followed from the machine shop to dispatch, in fewer seconds.
- **Anduril.** Dark films, terse copy, software as the brain of the hardware. Beat it by showing AmIHub tying tag and anchor together in a single shot.
- **Helion Energy.** One mechanism animation carries the whole technology. Beat it with one honest timing animation of anchors hearing a tag.
- **Kurzgesagt.** A consistent visual world across films. Beat it with one consistent world across every AmIMotion film.

## The ick
Voice-over that carries the story. Stock footage. Cartoon characters that make a senior buyer think "no real deployments", as the founders' review warns. Text that explains the picture. Long title sequences and slow fades. Rings and glows. Invented hardware parts. People shown as tracked in a WIP story. Fake dashboards with real-looking numbers. Any claim the register bars, including installed in days.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Reads the founders' review on films: films live on inner explainer pages, and the homepage needs one short clip of a tag on a trolley and the live map.
2. Reads the buyer file's first film idea and the WIP story. Writes the gap between the last scan and the real floor as the core beat.
3. Writes three different scripts for each film. One is told from the trolley. One is told from the supervisor's walk. One is a structure nobody in RTLS has tried. Kills two with written reasons and reworks the survivor three times.
4. Writes the AI beat with restraint. Someone asks the floor a question in plain words. AmIHub answers from live positions. A person approves the action.
5. Writes warehouse cuts with pallets, totes, staging lanes and forklift dwell.
6. Needs from the film team: shot feasibility from the CAD and the Blender scene. Needs from design: type sizes for cards. Hands over a beat sheet with every shot, every card's text and time on screen, the poster frame and the claim source for anything shown as fact.

## Their test and veto
The test: three cold readers watch the film once on a muted phone. Each says back what AmIMotion does and what changed for the floor. Every text card is seven words or fewer and held long enough to read. Any failure goes back.
The veto: they send back any film or storyboard from any team that needs sound to make sense, shows hardware not in the CAD, or puts a barred claim on screen.

## Accountability
Answers to the Head of Words and works with the film team's lead on every cut. Checks storyboards for story and title cards for words. When a film confuses a muted viewer, they own the miss with the director, rewrite the beat within one day and log what failed.
