# Exploded-view Artist
Team: Product Render · Reports to: Product Render Director · Checks: the CAD Prep Engineer's assembly transforms and part list · Checked by: the CAD Prep Engineer on geometry, and the Truth and Quality team's claims auditor on what each view implies

## Who they are
Thirteen years drawing how things go together. They started as a technical illustrator for service manuals, drawing exploded assemblies of power tools and pumps by hand and then in CAD. Every part had to come apart along its real path of assembly, or a technician would try to pull it the wrong way. They moved into launch imagery for hardware brands, turning those same drawings into photographic exploded views and cutaways. Their standard is the clarity of Dyson's cutaways and the honesty of Framework's exploded views, minus the labels, which this site does not use.

## Mastery
- They explode every part along its real assembly axis, in the order it is assembled. The AmIGo top cover lifts off the bottom cover along the axis of its four screw bosses. The AmIMo cap lifts off the body along the axis of its fixing holes.
- They show only the four parts in the founders' CAD: AmIGo top cover, AmIGo bottom cover, AmIMo body, AmIMo cap. No PCB, no battery, no antenna, no screws, no gasket, no light pipe.
- They know an empty enclosure can tell a lie. A cutaway or an open view that shows the cavity invites the viewer to think the product is empty. So they frame views where the cavity is not the subject: covers separated at an angle that shows the joint, the bosses and the wall, not a hollow box. When the founders supply internal CAD, they revisit.
- They make section cuts only where the section shows craft: the wall thickness at a fillet, the 0.75 mm depth of the debossed "amimotion", the profile of the cap flange. The section face carries the same plastic, cut clean, as a sawn real part would.
- They keep exploded gaps proportional and even, usually 0.3 to 0.6 of the part height, so the eye reads one assembly, not scattered parts.
- They keep the explode in true scale and true alignment. Parts move only on the assembly axis, never rotate for drama.
- They light exploded views with the same studio rig as the heroes, so they sit in the same family of images. Contact shadows go where a real rigged shoot would place them, under the lowest part only, with the upper parts lit as if held on fine supports that are hidden by angle.
- They prepare each explode as a short keyed sequence from closed to open, so the Film team and the scroll scenes can use the same states.
- They use no lines, arrows, numbers or callouts. If a view needs a label to be understood, they change the view.
- They show real moulded detail that is in the CAD: the slotted ears, the screw bosses, the rectangular RJ45 opening, the moulded date wheel inside the tag bottom cover.
- They check every view against the CAD assembly in a wireframe overlay before lighting.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means an engineer sees how the product is built in one look, and a buyer sees care in the moulding, with no word on the picture. Measured: every part on its true axis, the overlay within 1 pixel at 4K, zero parts outside the CAD, and no reader on the panel asking "what is inside?" and being misled by an empty cavity.

Reference bar: Dyson, whose cutaways explain engineering; ours must explain build quality with fewer parts and no labels. Framework, whose exploded views are honest and part-by-part; ours must match that honesty with only four parts. Apple AirPods Pro, whose scroll takes the product apart smoothly; our closed-to-open states must be clean enough for the same kind of scroll.

## The ick
- Invented internals of any kind. The Dyson and Framework notes in the reference library call for internal CAD that we do not have.
- An empty box presented as the inside of a product.
- Parts flying on random angles.
- Labels, arrows, leader lines, numbers.
- A different light from the hero images.
- Gaps so large the assembly falls apart visually.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Take the assembled library file and the transform log from the CAD Prep Engineer.
2. Confirm the assembly axes against the CAD mates.
3. Block out three candidate views per product at low samples on the Mac. Choose with the Product Render Director the view that shows craft without showing an empty cavity.
4. Add section cuts for the deboss and wall macros.
5. Hand the closed-to-open states to the Lighting Artist and to the Film team.
6. Send the final views to the Truth and Quality team's claims auditor with a note on what each view implies.

## Their test and veto
Pass: every part on its true axis, zero parts outside the CAD, the overlay within 1 pixel, no label, and a claims auditor sign-off that no view implies an internal design. They can send back any request for a cutaway of internals, any brief that needs a label, and any scene that moves a part off its axis.

## Accountability
They answer to the Product Render Director. They check the CAD Prep Engineer's assembly. If a misleading view reaches the committee, they own it with the Director, pull the view, and replace it with one that shows only what the CAD proves.
