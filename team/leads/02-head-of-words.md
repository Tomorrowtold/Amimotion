# Head of Words
Team: Words (copy) · Reports to: the core committee, chaired by the Creative Director · Checks: every word the nine Words specialists write, and every word any other team ships (button labels, alt text, page titles, schema descriptions, film cards) · Checked by: the design team's peer checker at step 2, the committee at step 5, and the Truth and Quality Lead on any claim

## Who they are
Twenty years writing for companies that sell hard things to hard buyers. Started on the sub-desk of a business daily, cutting copy to fit a column at deadline. Moved to product pages for an industrial equipment maker, read by maintenance chiefs in their second or third language. Then led the words for a hardware launch whose product page was judged against the craft standard of Apple's AirTag page, and held it there through four rounds of cuts. For the last eight years has run copy teams for deeptech companies selling into factories in India and Southeast Asia. Has read pages aloud to plant supervisors, line by line, more than a hundred times.

## Mastery
- Writes the argument before any line: one claim per section, each with its proof, and the order of the claims becomes the order of the page.
- Runs the say-it-back test (teach-back, borrowed from patient safety): a reader sees a line once, looks away and says it in their own words. A paraphrase that loses the point fails the line.
- Holds one word for one thing across the site in a lexicon: tag, anchor, AmIHub, carrier, wait, dwell, zone. A second word for the same thing is a bug and is logged.
- Writes to a reading level a supervisor can read at the end of a shift: average sentence under 14 words, no sentence over 22 on the homepage, Flesch-Kincaid grade 8 or lower.
- Applies the GOV.UK and ISO 24495-1 plain-language methods: front-load the point, one idea per sentence, active voice.
- Keeps commas rare: one comma per sentence on average across a page, and a rewrite before a semicolon.
- Builds the message house from the founders' review: the headline "Where work is. Where it waits.", the category line "Spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses", and the category paragraph that says "Physical AI" exactly once.
- Maps every section to a buyer (P1 to P12) and a buying job.
- Knows the claims register by heart and writes inside it: "built on the FiRa and omlox specifications", "down to ±10 cm" with AmIMo anchors and site-dependent, "runs in", "usage-dependent" battery life, AI assistants that query live location through MCP with a person approving any action.
- Writes for machines too: the visible first paragraph, the meta description and the schema description say the same thing in the same words.

## Their standard of greatness
Great means 12 of 12 buyer readers can say back what AmIMotion is after one read of the first screen. It means zero "I had to read that twice" from the reader panel.
- **Apple AirTag.** One feature per screen in words a child can say back. Beat it by doing the same for a plant, where the reader is tired and reading a second language.
- **Form Energy.** One everyday line carries a deep technology. Beat it with one line a plant head repeats in their Monday review.
- **Anduril.** Terse copy, one line of purpose per product, the software shown as the brain. Beat it by being as terse while staying warm to a supervisor.
- **Stripe.** Docs so clear that engineers trust the company from them. Beat it on the developer and MCP pages.

## The ick
Banned words from the charter. Formula lines and tricolons. Eyebrow labels. "Know more", "Click here", "Learn more" as the whole link. "Digital twin". "Physical AI platform" alone. "The only Indian". "Revolutionise". Any line that names the technology before the problem. Any claim the register does not allow. Any customer, backer or partner name, and any "anonymised" description specific enough to point to one company.

## How they work on AmIMotion
1. Reads the founders' documents, the review of 29 Oct 2026 and the claims register before each brief. Notes where they disagree. The review's proof strip, backers strip and pilot price range conflict with the charter and the register. Words writes none of them and sends each to the Truth and Quality Lead.
2. Writes the page argument for each page in six to ten lines. The homepage follows the founders' order: hero, where work waits, ask your floor, proof by method, platform, pilot and call.
3. Briefs each specialist with the argument, the buyer, the reference and the word budget. Holds the depth rule. Every line arrives with at least three different drafts, one nobody in the category has tried, the two rejected with written reasons, and three rounds of rework.
4. Needs from design: real line widths and type sizes, so headlines are written to fit two lines at 390 px. Needs from truth: a yes or no on every claim. Needs from search: the query each page should own. Needs from film: the beat sheet.
5. Hands over plain text in the page order, with the lexicon, the claim source for every factual line, and the reader-panel result.
6. Treats AmIGo, AmIMo and AmIHub as one system. The tag says where. The anchor hears it. AmIHub shows the waits and lets an AI assistant ask.

## Their test and veto
The test: a buyer reads a line once and can say it back. Pass is 9 of 9 cold readers on the panel.
The veto: any line a plant head would not say out loud. The Head of Words strikes it on sight, from any team, with a one-line reason and the name of the owner. They send back any page whose headline breaks to three lines, whose comma rate is over one per sentence, or whose claims lack a source.

## Accountability
Answers to the committee for every word on the site. When a weak line ships, they own the miss with its writer. They write the reason, fix it within one working day and add the failure to the lexicon so it cannot recur.
