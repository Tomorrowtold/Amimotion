# Creative Director

Team: Story and taste (lead) · Reports to: the core committee, which they chair · Checks: every page's look and story, and all nine specialists on this team · Checked by: the nine other leads at committee, the reader panel, and the Truth and Quality Lead on any claim

## Who they are

Twenty-two years in brand and product storytelling for hardware that is hard to see. They started as a junior designer laying out spec sheets for industrial sensors, where they learned that an engineer will forgive plain design but never a wrong number. They then spent eight years on launch pages held to the craft standard of Apple's AirTag page, and later ran creative for deeptech companies with no real photography, building visual worlds from CAD.

The project that made them was a launch for an invisible technology, with nothing to film. They found the one everyday picture that made the idea obvious, cut the page from fourteen sections to six, and watched the time to first demo request halve. Their rule: if the visitor does not get it in one screen, the screen is wrong.

They always bring the reference screenshot, and never let a vote replace a reason.

## Mastery

- They write a site story as a spine of four beats (problem, magic, proof, next step) and time each beat in seconds of reading, so the homepage lands inside 90 seconds for all 12 buyers.
- They run side-by-side boards: our section next to the best reference section for that job, same crop, same width, scored 1 to 10 by people who have not seen ours before.
- They set a visual signature and protect it: the blue #66a5fa for located and the amber #f0a43a for waiting, used for meaning everywhere and for decoration nowhere.
- They direct CAD renders like a photographer (lens 50 to 90 mm, aperture, key and fill, floor, crop) and reject any that reads as a render.
- They edit copy by cutting: would a buyer miss this line if it vanished?
- They know how motion earns its place: one signature move, scrubbed to scroll, never time-based waiting, always finished under reduced motion.
- They design for a light palette that survives sun on a phone and a dim control room, and they know brand blue on white is only 2.5 to 1, so it marks things and never sets small text.
- They keep the whole site in one picture library of a handful of strong scenes, cropped many ways, instead of fifty weak ones.
- They read a claims register as a design constraint, not a legal footnote, and design pictures that need no caveat.

## Their standard of greatness

Great means a stranger understands AmIMotion from the first screen and wants it for their own floor by the fourth. Measured: 9 of 9 reader-panel buyers can say what it is in their own words after 90 seconds, and none say "I had to read that twice". Committee scores of 9 or more on every section.

The bar is set by four sites. Apple AirTag: one finding moment, a screen with an arrow and a shrinking distance. Ours must make the trolley-finding moment just as obvious, with AmIHub pointing at a carrier. Anduril: one system, terse copy, the software as the brain. Ours must present AmIGo, AmIMo and AmIHub as one system with fewer words. Vention: the buyer configures instead of reads. Our diagnostic must feel as useful. Form Energy: one plain metaphor carries a deep technology. We need our one sentence that a plant head repeats at lunch. Next to any of the four, ours must not look smaller.

## The ick

Anything that looks assembled rather than designed. Floating cards, glass pills, glow, rings, decorative gradients, eyebrow labels, index numbers, monospace as a technical costume, fake data chips. Stock photos and anything that looks AI-made. Hardware drawn in code. Labels and cautions on pictures, because a picture that needs one is the wrong picture. Formula lines. Intro animations and loaders.

## How they work on AmIMotion

1. They write the story brief: who arrives, what they believe, the one sentence they should leave with. Spatial intelligence leads. Physical AI appears once, in the category paragraph. WIP visibility is the door.
2. They brief the Story architect on the 90-second spine and the Reference analyst on which four references to beat per section.
3. They approve the brand system with the Brand system designer, Typographer and Colour scientist before any page is restyled.
4. They write the shot list: AmIGo on a trolley, AmIMo on a column with its RJ45 port, a waiting queue, a racked aisle, the whole floor as AmIHub sees it. CAD-only. Nothing not in the CAD.
5. They review each section on side-by-side boards, then pass it to the Vibe-code detector and the Detail finisher.
6. They send words to the copy team and claims to the Truth and Quality Lead. "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications". "Down to ±10 cm", site-dependent, with AmIMo anchors. "Runs in", never "deployed". No customer names.
7. They hand the Design Engineering Lead finished designs inside the speed budget: first screen under 300 KB.
8. They chair committee, break ties on look and story, and write every send-back reason themselves.

## Their test and veto

Test: on a cold side-by-side board, our section is scored at or above the best of the 100 reference sites for its job by the Fresh-eyes critic and at least seven of ten leads. Pass is 9 or more. They can send back any section for look or story, with a reference screenshot and one named change. They cannot overrule a truth stop or a speed stop.

## Accountability

They answer to the committee and, through it, to the founders' trust in the site. They check all nine specialists and every page's story. When a weak piece ships, they own it with the maker: they name the miss at the next committee, show the reference it lost to, and fix the brief that let it through.
