## 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "AmIMotion is spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses: a tag on what moves, anchors on the columns that hear it, and one live map of where things went and how long they have waited."

Is that right? Yes. The category comes first and Physical AI is not in the opening. The chain is now complete: tag, then anchors that "hear its signal", then the map. "Where it went and how long it has waited" is more careful than v2's "where it is". It tells me the vendor knows a sleeping tag is not a live position, and that is a vendor I can defend in a steering committee. I still skip "You find out from a screen, not a walk." It is a plant-head line, not a board line, and it does no harm.

## 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** Same four words. "Your pilot on one page: To share with your team" is no longer singular to one plant, so it works for me too. "Robotics and AMRs: Robots and carriers on one map" is fine as location. If that page ever says "coordinate" or "dispatch", it goes back.
- **Hero.** Calm. The poster is still my "why" slide.
- **Screen 2, "A scan tells you where it passed. A tag tells you where it stopped."** I read the first half twice. "Where it passed" made me stop: passed which checkpoint, or passed inspection? v2's "where it was / where it is" was the cleaner board line. This version is more accurate, but less quotable. The body is for my supervisors, so I skim it. "It goes on the trolley, not on the person pushing it" will save me a works-committee conversation in two plants.
- **Screen 3, See where work waits.** "You name the zones" is still my template answer. "We agree with you how to link them before the pilot." It is honest, but it makes me ask whether we agree that once for the group or again in every plant. The page does not say.
- **Screen 4, Ask your floor.** This is now the best screen for me. "Asking changes nothing." Three words, and the board's AI question is answered without overclaim. "Any change, such as raising a work order, needs a person to approve it." The approval sits right next to the action example, which is correct. I read the small IT block, because AI readiness is my mandate. "The server is part of AmIHub, so it can run on a PC inside your plant." That is the fix I asked for, and I did not re-read it. "If the assistant runs inside your network too, nothing has to leave it." That is a condition, not a promise, and my IT head can sign it. "We show it live on a call" keeps it AMBER. No veto.
- **Screen 5, Three parts work as one system.** "Your team, your systems and AI assistants read the same live map." The one-data-model line is back on the scroll, right after the interfaces line, where I asked for it. With "open REST, MQTT, WebSocket and omlox hub interfaces" and "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications", this is the no-lock-in case, and it never says "lock-in". "Your MES stays in charge of every job" keeps my MES owner calm. I skip the 86-word anchor block, which is my OT head's.
- **Screen 6, Start with one bay.** "Then grow plant-wide on the same AmIHub. One AmIHub console can also run more than one site." The scale story now sits in the pilot story, not under the button. For the first time the page says my word, site. "Your team or your integrator can build on its open interfaces" balances "one contract". But the line I most want is also the line I trust least. It is a bare present-tense capability, and I am told nothing about what plant two reuses or costs us in effort. If it does not ship today, it is a plan shown as live, and that is my veto.
- **Footer.** "Group operations and digital: How it works." It is still a product page, not a rollout page. Now that /how-to-buy/ exists with "plant-wide on the same stack", that page would serve me better. Physical AI appears once, as direction ("the first layer of"). That passes.

Words I did not trust: "One AmIHub console can also run more than one site." I do not doubt the words. It carries the most weight for my role and has the least behind it on the page.
