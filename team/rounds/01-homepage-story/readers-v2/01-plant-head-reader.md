## 1. After the hero only

Say-back: "They put a small tag on trolleys, bins and tools and anchors on the columns, and one map shows where each one is and how long it has waited, so nobody walks the floor to find out."

Is it right? Yes, and I got it in about eight seconds without hearing the word "platform". The headline "Where work is. Where it waits." is the right four seconds. "You find out from a screen, not a walk." is still my walk at 7:40. The film is better than round 1's: there is one cut, the floor is coloured, then pale, then it fills in. Amber on the queue at inspection is my morning quality issue in one picture. "Spatial intelligence" is still the first noun after the brand, and it still means nothing to me. The next four sentences save it. "Anchors on your columns hear it." Hear what? I let it go again.

One worry: the hall is clean, a model with no wear, no tape and no grime. It does not lie that it is my floor, and I respect that. But B's worn yellow tape made me feel "these people have been on an Indian floor", and this one does not.

## 2. Screen by screen

- **Hero.** Clear and quiet. No numbers, no buttons, no hype. The colour-to-pale cut at 2 seconds might read as "before" or as a glitch for one loop, but by the second loop I get it.
- **Screen 2, "A scan tells you where it was. A tag tells you where it is."** This is the best screen on the page. It has A's sentence I would say at 8:30 and B's "The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." together. "Between scans, work moves and waits out of sight." That is the gap I live in. It does not call my barcodes useless, so my MES head will not get defensive. A real AmIHub screen recording is the thing I would trust most, if it ships. If it is the fallback still, it is just one blue trolley on a drawing, which is fine but weak.
  - Nothing read twice.
- **Screen 3, "See where work waits."** "A long wait shows you where to look. Why it waited stays your team's call." I trust this. No vendor that promises nothing on diagnosis is lying to me. "You name the zones." Good, it is my floor, my words. Three trolleys amber in the queue, one being worked on: I understood the picture before the copy.
  - Nothing read twice.
- **Screen 4, "Ask your floor a question."** "Which trolleys have waited at inspection for more than an hour?" I would ask that tomorrow. "We show it live on a call" is honest. The MCP block is smaller and now says what MCP is, so I can skip it and know my IT head will read it.
  - Did not trust: "A person approves any action." What action? Until now this was a map. Now an assistant can do things on my floor? That raises a question it does not answer. Either say what action, or cut the line from my part of the screen.
  - Words I don't know: "MCP" (now defined, so I let it pass).
- **Screen 5, "Three parts work as one system."** The tag and anchor blocks are the clearest hardware text so far. They screw on, the battery lasts 1 to 3 years, they are 3 to 8 m up, they need only power, and "We survey your floor and plan the anchors." That last line answers "what do I have to do": not much. "Your MES stays in charge." is good again.
  - Read twice: "Works out where each tag is, down to ±10 cm with AmIMo anchors." With whose anchors, then? It reads as if there are other anchors. Then "Your site sets the final figure." I understood it on the second read, and I like the honesty, but it took two reads.
  - Words I don't know or don't need: "PoE", "REST, MQTT and WebSocket", "FiRa and omlox". They are for my OT and IT heads. They are at least contained in one block and one line now, not on my decision screen.

That last line is the one my HR head and union need, and it is new. Good. "What you buy" is a straight list with "plus our survey of your floor", plus one contract and one support line.   - "Running more than one plant? Ask us how the second one starts." It is not for me today, but my COO runs three, so it is fine.
  - Still missing: who comes to my bay when it breaks. "One support line" is a phone number, not an engineer at 2 am.
- **Footer.** "Plant heads: How a pilot runs." I know where to go. Physical AI is down here once, in the company paragraph, not between the hardware and the pilot. That is what I asked for in round 1. Good. NXP and FiRa sit here too, where my OT engineer will find them and I will not trip on them.

## 7. Best and worst lines

- **Best:** "A scan tells you where it was. A tag tells you where it is." (screen 2). This is the sentence I would say at tomorrow's 8:30 meeting, word for word.
- **Worst:** "A person approves any action." (screen 4). It is short and safe-sounding, but it implies the assistant takes actions on my floor and never says which ones. My COO would ask "what actions?" and I would have no answer.

## 8. One change that makes it a 10

Fill the empty stop slot on screen 6, after "You choose the flow you want to see.", with the checks in floor words. For example: "First check: your supervisor walks the bay with our engineer and the map matches. Second check: your team uses it in the morning meeting for two weeks. If either fails, you stop there." Even the old B line, "Each stage has a check before the next, and you can stop at any of them," would put me back at 8. Naming the checks makes it a 10.
