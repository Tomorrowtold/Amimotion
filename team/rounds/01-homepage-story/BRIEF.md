# Round 1 brief: the homepage story

## The job
Write the AmIMotion homepage as a story, screen by screen: the exact words and a precise description of the picture or film on each screen. Words and pictures only. No code yet.

## What Shiv asked for (1 Nov 2026, his words condensed)
- The homepage is an explainer of what the company is: value propositions only.
- Within 90 seconds every buyer persona is absolutely clear what AmIMotion is, and believes "this is what I want for me".
- Explain through visuals and great copy. Humanly written. Few commas. Each buyer gets it in one go, without explanation; if they want more they can learn immediately.
- No gimmicks. Nothing that does not serve the purpose. No waiting. No filler: every inch adds meaning.
- Light, beautiful colours that buyers like, brand blue. Must not look vibe coded.
- Hardware only as photoreal renders from the founders' CAD (no shoot possible). No stock or AI-looking pictures. No "simulation" labels anywhere, so no picture that would need one.
- The best nav in the world for humans, AEO and GEO; simple to navigate and be guided.
- Must beat every one of the 100 reference sites.

## Hero direction Shiv already gave (keep unless the team finds clearly better, and say why)
- Left: crisp, technically correct, human copy explaining what AmIMotion is. "Spatial intelligence" is the word. No buttons in the hero. "Book a call" lives only in the nav until the third act.
- Right: a before/after "magic" film. Before: the plant as it is today, work invisible between scan points. After: the AmIGo tag connects carriers (core), parts and assemblies, tools and fixtures, vehicles, robots, and visible flow appears. Clear within 1 second. No people tracking in the hero, no tech numbers in the hero.
- Shiv rejected before: data cards or chips like "Trolley 14 · Waiting 42 min" (looked vibe coded), rings, glow, radio pulses, plants drawn as grey boxes, CSS-3D hardware, formula lines, 60-word headlines, long verbose homepages with jargon (C-014, J-208).
- Earlier wins Shiv liked: the floor diagnostic simulation as the main lead magnet; "Where work is. Where it waits." as H1 (founders' review keeps it); value props as buyer outcomes (pain, gain, why us), not feature one-liners.
