## 0. The picture rule (applies to all three concepts)

Shiv's ruling: CAD renders of real hardware and clearly designed drawings are fine. A fake plant passed off as real is not. K03 ("Labels for creative work") says a simulated scene needs a "Simulation" label. So a scene that shows AmIHub's output (trails, waits, answers) on a plant that looks photographed is a simulated scene. It would need the label. It is out.

What is allowed:

1. **Hardware renders from the founders' CAD**, photoreal, including in context: tag on a trolley rail, anchor on a column. FR D7 asks for exactly this. Only what is in the CAD. No mounts, brackets, PCBs, batteries or PCs. No deboss, port or tab that the CAD does not have.
2. **A clearly designed world.** The exact art direction that makes a floor picture allowed:
   > The hall is a designed model, never a photograph. One flat matte material per kind of thing. Simplified shapes. No wear, grime, tyre marks, patched slabs or faded paint. No daylight realism, sun patches or depth of field on wide shots. No people. No signs, text, brands or numbers. Blue trails and amber tints are flat graphic marks. Nothing in it suggests a real or named site.
3. **Real AmIHub screen captures**, unedited, from AmIMotion's own demo floor or a site cleared in writing. Only features that are live (C18, Q4, Q6). No customer detail. Demo trackable names must match the real objects on that demo floor, so no staged plant is passed off as a site.

What is out: any photoreal plant, any "honest wear" realism, any floor that "looks real" to a buyer, and any shot that calls a rendered overlay "AmIHub".

Concept A's world ("a real place in model form") can meet rule 2 with the art direction above, so its floor pictures are REWORD. Concepts B and C ask for a photoreal plant on purpose (B: "P11 sees an Indian plant that looks real"; C: "honest wear", "the biggest risk is the plant render reading as CGI"). Their floor pictures are STOP. Rebuilt under rule 2, they come back for a fresh check.
