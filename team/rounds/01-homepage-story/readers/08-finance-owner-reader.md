# Reader 08: Finance and Owner (P9 procurement, P11 promoter). Round 1, homepage

SIMULATION. I am not a real buyer. I am a chartered accountant who has sat beside a promoter for nine years, reading on a phone between a bank call and a budget review. Two voices: **Procurement** ("what am I buying, what recurs, who supports it") and **Promoter** ("who are these people, will they come when it breaks").

Read: section 3 copy and picture descriptions, section 4 nav. Nothing else.

What I hunt for on every screen: the parts I am buying named separately (tags, anchors, software, deployment), a pricing line that is honest, one contract and one support line, made in India as a strength, a pilot I can stop, and a face.

---

## Concept A

### 1. After the hero only
"AmIMotion sells small tags you stick on things that move, anchors you fix on your columns, and a live map that shows where each thing is and how long it has stood still."

Right. It is the only hero of the three that tells procurement there is hardware in two parts plus software. I could write a rough BOQ heading from it. It does not say who installs it, but a hero should not.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** Procurement: "Pilot" as a top word is good, I know where to click. Promoter: "Company" is the place I would look for faces. Fine. "Search-time calculator": I do not trust a vendor calculator that works out my losses for me.
- **Hero.** Procurement: three parts named in 38 words. Good. Promoter: a film of a hall with no people and no Indian detail. It is clean, but it could be anywhere.
- **Where work waits.** Procurement: "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is." I understood it first time. Promoter: block 3 ends "One team in India designs, builds and supports every part." That is the sentence my MD wants. It is buried at the bottom of the third block, where he will not reach it on a phone.
- **Ask your floor.** Procurement: "A person approves any action" and "Every call is logged" cover the obligations question. Promoter: he will like that the board's AI question is answered. "MCP" means nothing to either of us.
  - Read twice: "The server runs where AmIHub runs, even on a PC in your plant with no internet." (16 words). Which server? I thought AmIHub was the server.
  - Did not know: "MCP", "MCP server".
- **One system.** Procurement: this is the line-item screen and it nearly works. Tag, anchor, AmIHub, each with one job. "Anchors need only power" tells me there is no cabling scope hiding. But deployment and survey are not named as a part here, so I still do not know if installation is in the price or a separate line.
  - Read twice: "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications, with NXP Trimension UWB silicon inside." (13 words). Five names I cannot check, in one line.
  - Read twice: "Every claim on this site says what it rests on." (10 words). It sounds like a promise from a legal team. I would test it before I believed it.
  - Did not know or did not trust: "FiRa", "omlox", "NXP Trimension", "UWB", "±10 cm" (whose test?).
  - Trust check: "Runs in automotive, robotics and warehousing plants." This is the allowed form. Good. No names, no logos. Good.
- **Start with one bay.** Procurement: "We survey the floor and plan the anchors. You bring the flow you want to see and one contact on the floor." This is the best obligations sentence on any of the three pages. I know what my company owes. Promoter: there is no stop. One bay, then "grow plant-wide". Nothing says I can walk away after the first bay. A cautious approver needs that sentence before he signs anything.
- **Footer.** Procurement: "Procurement and finance: How a pilot runs." At least I am named. But I want "How to buy", not the pilot page. Promoter: owners are lumped with plant heads. There is no "Who we are" row. No founder anywhere on the page.

### 3. Where I would have stopped
I would not stop. I would slow down at screen 4 on the FiRa line and skip to screen 5. The promoter would stop at screen 3: by then he has seen two screens of floor and no people behind the company.

### 4. Question still open
Procurement: "Is the survey and deployment a separate line, and what do I pay every year after the pilot?" Promoter: "Who started this company, and can I meet them?"

### 5. Score
**6 / 10.** Clear on what is bought. No stop option, no face, no pricing line.

## Concept B

### 1. After the hero only
"AmIMotion puts a small tag on each trolley, bin and tool, and a live map shows where it is and how long it has waited, so my people stop walking the floor to find things."

Mostly right. Procurement knows there is a tag and a map. The anchors are missing from the copy. They only appear in the film, as the camera passes a column. So my first BOQ from this hero would be wrong: it would leave out the anchors and the cabling question.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** Procurement: "Your floor" is clever but I do not know that the pilot is under it. Under "Company" I find "How to buy". That is where I would go, but I had to open menus to find it. "What searching costs you": same distrust as in A. That is a savings calculator by another name.
- **Hero.** Procurement: "You find out from a screen, not a walk." I understood it. But it is the nearest any page gets to a saving claim, and it sits in the hero. Promoter: "an Indian component plant", worn yellow aisle tape, 7:40 in the morning. He would say "that is our plant". This is the only film that makes "made in India" a strength without saying the words.
- **Find it.** Procurement: no new line items. Promoter: "The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." He has done that walk on a plant visit and been angry. It lands. A real screen capture, not a mock-up, earns trust with me.
- **Ask your floor.** Procurement: "A person approves any action, and every request is logged." That covers obligations. "We show it live on the call" is honest: it tells me the AI part is a demo, not a promise. Good.
  - Did not know: "MCP server".
- **How it knows.** Procurement: this is the best line-item screen of the three. Three parts, bold names, one job each, and a battery life with "depending on use". I know what recurs: batteries. I know "Needs only power. No data cables to run." Then "One team builds every layer, so you have one contract and one support line." That is exactly my two procurement questions answered in one sentence.
  - Read twice: "Every trolley tells the room where it is." (8 words). Tells the room? I had to stop and translate it to "every trolley reports its position".
  - Read twice: "This is spatial intelligence: the exact place and time of everything that moves in your plant, joined with jobs and zones." (21 words). It is a definition, not a sentence for me.
  - Did not know or did not trust: "Physical AI", "FiRa", "omlox", "±10 cm depending on your site".
  - Promoter: "Designed and built in India" is here, but it is the fourth sentence of a dense paragraph. He will not reach it.
- **Start with one bay.** Procurement: "We survey your floor and plan the anchors." So survey is a part. "Each stage has a check before the next, and you can stop at any of them." This is the sentence I have been looking for on every vendor site for nine years. It lets my MD say yes to a small step. Promoter: he relaxes here.
  - Did not trust: "Which walk would you cut first?" (6 words). It is a question, but it is a sales question. It invites me to put a saving number on the call. Near the money button, it reads like the start of an ROI pitch.
- **Footer.** Procurement: "Procurement and finance: How to buy." Named, and sent to the right page. Promoter: "Owners and MDs: About us." The only concept that gives the promoter his own row. Still no face on the homepage itself.

### 3. Where I would have stopped
I would not stop. Screen 4 is long, but it is the screen I came for. The promoter might drift during screen 2 (it is the supervisor's screen, not his) and come back at screen 5.

### 4. Question still open
Procurement: "What does the deployment cost as a line, and what is the AMC after year one?" Promoter: "Who are the founders, and will they be on this call?"

### 5. Score
**7 / 10.** It answers "what am I buying", "who supports it" and "can I stop". It loses points for the anchors missing from the hero, for no pricing line, for no face, and for "Which walk would you cut first?"

## Concept C

### 1. After the hero only
"AmIMotion is spatial intelligence software that shows where every tagged trolley, pallet and tool is and how long it has waited."

Half right. It reads as software only. "Tagged" hints there are tags, but nothing says who supplies them or that there are anchors. Procurement cannot name what is being bought from this hero. And "Spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses" as the first words is a category name, not an answer.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** Procurement: "Start small" is the best nav word on any of the three. It tells my MD the risk is small before he clicks. But there is no "How to buy" anywhere in the nav or footer. Pricing, contract and payment terms have no home. "What searching costs you": same distrust.
- **Hero.** Procurement: no parts named. Promoter: the split floor down an aisle line is easy to see. "Two people may walk the floor": if they look fake, he will notice.
- **Your trolleys say where they are.** Procurement: here are the three parts, finally, in screen 2. "No data or sync cables to run. Anchors need only power, DC or PoE." Good: no cabling scope hidden. Battery life with "depending on use": good, I know what recurs.
  - Did not know: "PoE", "sync cables".
  - Promoter: real hardware on a real trolley. He likes things he can hold.
- **See where work waits.** Procurement: nothing for me, and that is fine. Promoter: "Why it waited stays your team's call." He trusts a vendor who says what it does not do.
  - Did not know: "zone entry" (I know what a zone is; I do not know how a zone gets made, or who makes it).
- **Ask your floor.** Procurement: "Each assistant gets its own access, and every call is logged." Good.
  - Read twice: "This is spatial intelligence. AmIHub keeps the place and time of everything tagged, joined with your zones, so people, your systems and AI assistants read the same floor." (28 words). Too long for a phone. "Read the same floor" is not a phrase I would repeat to anyone.
  - Did not know: "Physical AI", "MCP server".
- **Start with one bay.** Procurement: the commercial facts are all here, in one list: "Designed and built in India. One team, one contract, one support line." "Runs in automotive manufacturing, robotics and warehousing." Both are allowed lines. But they are stacked under a spec line my MD will never read.
  - Read twice: "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications. Talks to MES, ERP and WMS over REST, MQTT and WebSocket." (18 words). That is a line for the IT head, put in the middle of the promoter's screen.
  - Read twice: "When it earns its place, it grows across the plant on the same AmIHub." (14 words). Who decides it has earned its place? The vendor? Me? There is no gate and no stop.
  - Missing: no survey, no "you bring one contact", no stop. Procurement cannot see the obligations on either side.
- **Footer.** Procurement: no row for procurement, no row for owners, no "How to buy", no pricing line. A plain index. Promoter: "About us" is in the last column. That is all.

### 3. Where I would have stopped
Procurement would stop at the hero. I cannot tell if this is a software subscription or a hardware supply, and I would have to scroll to find out. A busy procurement head closes the tab there. The promoter would carry on to screen 2 because the hardware is real, then stop at screen 5 when it turns into REST and MQTT.

### 4. Question still open
Procurement: "Who installs this, what does that cost, and how do I buy it?" Promoter: "Who is behind this company?"

### 5. Score
**5 / 10.** The parts and the support line are all on the page, but in the wrong order, with no buying path, no obligations and no stop.

## 6. Ranking

**B, A, C.**
B is the only one where a cautious approver can say yes: three parts with one job each, "one contract and one support line", a survey, and "you can stop at any of them".
A names the parts best in the hero and gives the clearest obligations line ("You bring the flow... one contact on the floor"), but has no stop and hides "built in India" in block 3.
C is the most beautiful on paper, but the hero does not say what is bought and the footer has no way to buy.

## 7. Best and worst line

**Best:** "Each stage has a check before the next, and you can stop at any of them." (B, screen 5). This one sentence is what lets a promoter sign a small cheque.

**Worst:** "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications, with NXP Trimension UWB silicon inside." (A, screen 4). Five names that neither procurement nor the promoter can check, in one line, at the exact screen where we are deciding whether to trust you. It borrows trust from brands instead of earning it. (C's "Talks to MES, ERP and WMS over REST, MQTT and WebSocket" is a close second, for the same reason.)

## 8. One change to make B a 10

Add one short "What you buy" block to screen 5, above the two buttons, and put a face next to it:

> **What you buy.** AmIGo tags. AmIMo anchors. AmIHub. And our survey, setup and support on your floor. One contract, one support line, from one team in Bengaluru. Prices are shared under NDA, after the survey.
>
> On the call: one of the people who built it. [photo and name, public details or written approval only]

This names deployment as its own part, which no concept does. It puts "prices under NDA" in plain words, so "Book a call" is not a hidden quote request. It gives the promoter a face. And at the same time, change "Which walk would you cut first?" to "See it on real data first." so that nothing near the money button reads like a saving claim.

## Flags for the Buyer Truth Lead and the Truth and Quality Lead

- **All three: no pricing line.** No concept says "prices are shared under NDA". "Book a call" becomes "Contact us for pricing" with nothing else, which is my ick.
- **All three: deployment services are not named as a separate part.** A and B mention the survey in passing. C does not mention it.
- **All three: founders and engineers are invisible.** The pass test ("the promoter asks to meet the founders") fails on every concept. Bios are AMBER: public details or written approval only.
- **All three nav or footer: "Search-time calculator" (A) and "What searching costs you" (B, C).** Check that this tool does not output a saving, payback or ROI figure. That would be NEVER in file 03.
- **B hero: "You find out from a screen, not a walk."** This is close to a saving claim. B has already sent it to the Truth Lead. I agree it needs a ruling.
- **B screen 5: "Which walk would you cut first?"** It invites a savings conversation at the button. Suggest a change.
- **A and C: no stop option on the pilot.** Only B has one, and B marks it PROPOSAL. If the founders do not confirm a gated pilot, B loses its best line. That needs to be settled first.
- **A, B, C: "Your MES stays in charge."** All three mark it PROPOSAL. It is a commercial obligation. Confirm it before it ships.
- **Deployment line.** A: "Runs in automotive, robotics and warehousing plants." C: "Runs in automotive manufacturing, robotics and warehousing." Both are the allowed form. Both are flagged for live-site confirmation. B does not use it on the homepage.
- **No RED found.** No funding, revenue, traction count, investor name, customer name, logo or testimonial in any concept.
