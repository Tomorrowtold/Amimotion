# Reader panel, round 1: Plant Head (P1)

SIMULATION. I am a hypothesis built from kit 09, not an interviewed buyer.

The reader: plant head of a 1,200-person Tier 1 axle plant in Manesar. I read on my phone at 8:15 pm in the car, after three unread vendor emails. I read section 3 (screens) and section 4 (nav) only.

---

## Concept A: "the homepage after Shiv's hero"

### 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "They put a small tag on trolleys and things, anchors on the columns, and a map shows where each one is and how long it has stood still."

Is that right? Yes, and I got there without the word "platform". But I got it from the copy, not the film. The film's "before" needs me to know what the little blue ticks at the "scan desks" mean. On a phone I see pale, then colour, then a macro, all with hard cuts. In ten seconds that reads as a showreel. "Spatial intelligence" means nothing to me. I skip it and read the next sentence. "Anchors on your columns hear it": hear what? It is radio. I let it go.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "Platform" is the first word in the bar. That is the vendor's word, not mine. "Uses" is a strange word. Who says "Uses"?
- **Hero.** Copy is clear: four short sentences, no hype. The film is busy for a phone: three hard cuts in 12 seconds.
- **Screen 2, "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is."** This one hit. It is the sentence I would say at the 8:30 meeting. The lead is good: "So someone walks the floor to find it." That is my floor. Then three blocks, each with a title, a pain, a gain, a "why us" and a link. On a phone that is a lot of stacked text, and I read the lead and block 3 only.
  - Read twice: "Now every tagged carrier, pallet, tote, tool and forklift shows on one map." It is a catalogue, and over 15 words.
  - Read twice: "One team in India designs, builds and supports every part." It is good news in the wrong block. Why is it under "the whole plant on one map"?
  - "The plan says one thing and the floor does another." That is plan versus actual in my words. Good.
  - "Finding out why stays your team's call." I trust this. A vendor who says it will not diagnose for me is not lying to me.
- **Screen 3, Ask your floor.** "Which forklifts have been idle for more than an hour, and where are they?" I would ask that. Then: "AmIHub has an MCP server built in. Any assistant that speaks MCP can use it." I do not know MCP. I do not trust a word I cannot repeat to my COO. Three lines on access, logs and no internet are for my IT head, not for me. I skimmed.
- **Screen 4, One system.** "One team builds every layer." Good headline. "Down to ±10 cm with AmIMo anchors. Your site sets the final figure." Honest, and I like that it does not promise.
  - Words I don't know: "FiRa", "omlox", "NXP Trimension UWB silicon". My OT engineer will want them. I do not.
  - Don't trust: "Runs in automotive, robotics and warehousing plants." Which plants? My COO's first question is "who else runs it?" An unnamed list reads like a list from a pitch deck.
  - "Every claim on this site says what it rests on." Strong, if it is true. I would check one.
- **Screen 5, Start with one bay.** "You bring the flow you want to see and one contact on the floor." This answers "what do I have to change", so it is the best pilot line in A. "Your MES stays in charge." Good. But there is no gate and no way to stop. A pilot with no stop is a pilot I have killed twice before. "Before anyone calls you" is respectful. "Not slides" is right.
- **Footer.** "Plant heads and owners: How a pilot runs." Good, I know where to go. The Physical AI paragraph is down here, where it belongs.

### 3. Where I would stop
I would slow down at screen 3 (MCP) and stop at screen 4 when I hit FiRa, omlox and NXP. I would come back only if someone forwarded me screen 5.

### 4. My question at the end
"Can I stop it, and at what point, and what does stopping cost me?"

### 5. Score
**6 / 10.** It has the best single sentence for my meeting. But it makes me read too much and never tells me I can stop.

## Concept B: "the buyer's morning"

### 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "A tag on each trolley, bin and tool, and a map that shows where it is and how long it has waited, so my people stop walking the floor to find it."

Is that right? Yes. That is my problem named exactly, in the hero. "You find out from a screen, not a walk." I said it back in eight seconds. The film starts at eye level, where I cannot see my own floor, and climbs. That is what my walk at 7:40 feels like. The floor looks Indian: worn yellow tape, CNC enclosures, steel trolleys. It plays once and holds, so it does not keep moving while I read.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "How it works · Where it helps · Your floor · Company." Four plain words. "Your floor" is the only label on any of the three that talks to me.
- **Hero.** Felt like someone had been on a floor. "Spatial intelligence" still means nothing to me, but the next three sentences carry it.
- **Screen 2, "Find it from a screen, not a walk."** "Think of the last time someone went looking for a trolley. The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." Yes. The second walk is the real one. A real AmIHub screen capture, not a drawing, is the thing I would trust most on the page. The headline repeats the hero's last line. That's fine for me, it sticks.
- **Screen 3, Ask your floor.** "Which trolleys have waited at inspection for more than an hour?" That is my quality issue from this morning. Good.
  - Didn't trust: "so the assistant your team already uses can connect." My team uses no assistant. This line assumes I am behind and makes me feel it.
  - Don't know: "MCP".
  - "We show it live on the call." Good. That is how to say "it is not ready to click yet" honestly.
- **Screen 4, "Every trolley tells the room where it is."** I read the headline twice. "Tells the room" is cute, and I do not talk like that. The three blocks are the clearest hardware text across all three concepts: screws or sticks, 1 to 3 years of battery, 3 to 8 m up, only power. "Your MES stays in charge" again.
  - Over 15 words, and read twice: "This is spatial intelligence: the exact place and time of everything that moves in your plant, joined with jobs and zones." It says "everything that moves", but it is only what you tag. My COO would catch that.
  - Read twice: "Designed and built in India and built on the FiRa and omlox specifications." Built, and built.
  - Ick: "It is the first layer of Physical AI for factories and warehouses." This is an investor line in the middle of my page, between the hardware and the pilot.
  - "One team builds every layer, so you have one contract and one support line." Good. But does a support line mean an engineer in my bay at 2 am?
- **Screen 5, "Start with one bay. Grow to the plant."** "Each stage has a check before the next, and you can stop at any of them." That is the line I came for, and only B has it. It does not say what the checks are. "Which walk would you cut first?" is a good question to put before a call. "Your bay builds itself as you answer" is a gimmick, but a harmless one.
- **Footer.** "Plant head: How a pilot runs." And "Automation and OT: How it works on a steel floor." Someone knows my OT head's first objection.

### 3. Where I would stop
I would not stop. I would slow down at screen 4's category paragraph, then pick up again at screen 5.

### 4. My question at the end
"What exactly are the checks, who signs them off, and who else is running this today?"

### 5. Score
**8 / 10.** I would forward it to my COO tonight, with one line from me: "Ask them what the checks are."

## Concept C: "One floor, seen from five heights"

### 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "Software that shows where tagged trolleys, pallets and tools are on my floor and how long they have waited."

Is that right? Half. The hero does not say they put a tag on anything or sell hardware. "Tagged" is doing the work, and I could read this as software on top of my barcodes. "AmIMotion shows where every tagged trolley, pallet and tool is, and how long it has waited." That is 18 words. The split frame along the aisle line is the best one-second picture of the three: nothing on one side, blue and amber on the other. But there are "two people may walk the floor, small and unmarked." The first thing my union and HR will ask is "is it tracking the operators?" Don't put people in the frame if you do not want that question.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "How it works · Where it helps · Start small · Company." "Start small" is good. It tells me the risk is small before I click.
- **Hero.** The picture is strong. The copy says less than A or B.
- **Screen 2, "Your trolleys say where they are."** Clear. "An AmIGo tag rides on each trolley, pallet or tote. AmIMo anchors on the columns hear it. AmIHub puts it on the map." That is three product names in three sentences, and I have to learn them all before I care. "No data or sync cables to run. Anchors need only power, DC or PoE." Words for my electrical in-charge: "sync", "PoE". The specs line up honestly.
- **Screen 3, "See where work waits."** This is the best section on my problem in any concept. "You see how long a trolley sat at inspection, or a pallet in a staging lane. A long wait shows you where to look. Why it waited stays your team's call." There are no promises. On a mid phone, a 48-frame scrub tied to my thumb is going to stutter. The link text "Where it helps" tells me nothing about where it goes.
- **Screen 4, Ask your floor.** The same inspection question as B. Good.
  - Over 15 words, and read twice: "AmIHub keeps the place and time of everything tagged, joined with your zones, so people, your systems and AI assistants read the same floor." That is 25 words.
  - "It is the first layer of Physical AI for factories and warehouses." It is in the middle of the page again.
  - "MCP" again, unexplained.
- **Screen 5, "Start with one bay."** "When it earns its place, it grows across the plant on the same AmIHub." I like "earns its place". Who decides it has earned it? There is no gate and no stop. Then, right where I am deciding, comes a stack: PC with no internet, FiRa, omlox, REST, MQTT, WebSocket, built in India, "runs in automotive manufacturing, robotics and warehousing". That is my IT head's checklist on my decision screen.
- **Footer.** A plain index. Nothing says "plant head, go here". That's fine, but less than A or B.

### 3. Where I would stop
I would read through screen 3 happily, slow down at screen 4, and lose interest at screen 5 when the pilot turned into protocol names.

### 4. My question at the end
"Do you sell me the tags and anchors and put them in, or is this software I put on top of something?" Also: "Can I stop?"

### 5. Score
**6 / 10.** It has the best picture and the best paragraph on waiting, and the weakest hero copy and the weakest close.

## 6. Ranking
**B, then A, then C.**
- B names my walk in the hero, uses a real screen and not a drawing, and is the only concept that says I can stop.
- A has the sentence I would repeat at my meeting ("Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is."), but it buries me in blocks and gives me no exit.
- C has the best picture and the best paragraph on waiting, but its hero hides the hardware and its pilot screen is written for my IT head.

## 7. Best and worst lines
- **Best:** "Think of the last time someone went looking for a trolley. The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." (B, screen 2). A close second is "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is." (A, screen 2). B's line makes me feel it. A's line is the one I would say out loud.
- **Worst:** "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications. Talks to MES, ERP and WMS over REST, MQTT and WebSocket." (C, screen 5). It is the wrong reader at the moment I decide. The runner-up is "so the assistant your team already uses can connect" (B, screen 3). It assumes I have one, and I would look naive repeating it.

## 8. One change that makes B a 10
On screen 5, name the checks in floor words. For example: "Check 1: the map matches your bay when your supervisor walks it with our engineer. Check 2: your team uses it in the morning meeting for two weeks. If either fails, you stop." That one sentence answers "How do I know it worked?", "Can I stop?" and "Who comes?" together. (If it is cheap, also move the Physical AI line from screen 4 to the footer.)

## Tests
- Ten-second test: A pass, B pass, C partial (the hero never says there is a tag or hardware).
- Forward test: B yes, to my COO. A and C no.
- Morning-meeting sentence (from B, with A's line): "There's a tag-and-map setup that shows where every trolley is and how long it's waited. We could try one bay, and they say we can stop at any check."
- Pass rule ("no line read twice"): all three fail. Each has at least two lines I read twice.
