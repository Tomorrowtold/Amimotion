# Reader panel, round 1: Industrial Engineer Reader (P3)

SIMULATION. Hypothesis buyer from kit 09 section 6, not an interview. Read cold on a phone after a shift, section 3 copy and pictures plus the nav only. Then a second pass to audit definitions only.

The one thing I read for: can I say, in 90 seconds, what this measures and how it defines a wait? Entry event, exit event, time window, what happens to a carrier still in the zone at shift end. No concept gives me all four. One gets me two.

---

## Concept A: "Where work is. Where it waits." (scan gap)

### 1. After the hero only
"A tag on each moving thing and anchors on the columns give one live map of where it is now and how long it has stood still."
Mostly right. But "stood still" is not "waits", and the headline says waits. A trolley standing still at station 2 while the operator works on it is not waiting. It is value-added time. The hero mixes idle and dwell in its first paragraph. I noticed it on the first read.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** Plain. "Learn" holds "How waits are timed". Good, that is my page and it has my words in its name.
- **Hero.** The film's "before" has scan desks that leave a blue tick and nothing between them. That is exactly how I explain MES data to a plant head: point events, nothing between. I believed it. Then the copy says "how long it has stood still". Read twice: "One live map shows where each thing is now and how long it has stood still." (17 words). Stood still where? In a zone? Since when?
- **Where work waits.** Headline "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is." Clean. The block "Every wait, timed." is the best thing I read across all three: "Each move into and out of a zone gets a time." That is an entry and an exit. "You name the zones and set the limits." That is the zone model in my hands. "Finding out why stays your team's call." Honest. The picture is a queue zone in front of a station, outlined, separate from the station. Someone here knows a queue is not a process. Still missing: shift end, open visits, boundary.
  Read twice: "One team in India designs, builds and supports every part." (10 words). It lands at the end of a block about flow across bays. Wrong place, I went back to check I had not missed a link.
  The "whole plant" still is a spaghetti diagram of trails "from the last hour" according to the brief, but nothing on the page says the window. A trail picture with no time window is the first thing I ask about.
- **Ask your floor.** "Which forklifts have been idle for more than an hour, and where are they?" Concrete. A threshold and a unit. I can test that. "Idle" for a forklift I accept. Words I do not know: MCP. I skipped the three MCP lines.
- **One system.** "Down to ±10 cm with AmIMo anchors. Your site sets the final figure." Correct shape. I trust that more than a bare number. Words I did not know: FiRa, omlox, Trimension. I do not need them, the OT engineer does. "Runs in automotive, robotics and warehousing plants." Which plants, how long? I do not count this as proof.
- **Start with one bay.** Small, bounded, "Your MES stays in charge". Fine. But there is no baseline. A pilot "watches one flow", then "grow plant-wide". Grow on what evidence? Watched against what? This is where I would expect "first we measure today's waits, then we compare". Nothing.
- **Footer.** "Lean and IE leads: How waits are timed". Somebody wrote a row for me. I would click it.

### 3. Where I would stop
I would not stop. I would leave at Screen 2 through "How waits are timed", because that link is the only place my real question can be answered, and the homepage has told me just enough to make me want it.

### 4. Question I still have
"A carrier enters the queue zone at 21:50 and is still there at 22:00 shift end. Is that a wait of ten minutes, an open visit, or dropped from the report? And what baseline does the pilot measure before anything changes?"

### 5. Score
**7/10.** I would forward Screen 2 block 2 to my production manager today. Not the whole page, because the pilot has no baseline and he will ask me for one.

## Concept B: the buyer's morning (the walk)

### 1. After the hero only
"A tag on each trolley, bin and tool puts it on a live map with how long it has waited, so you find it from a screen instead of walking."
Right for a supervisor. For me it is a finding tool, not a measuring tool. "How long it has waited" has no zone and no start.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "How it works · Where it helps · Your floor · Company". "Your floor" is good. I have to guess where dwell definitions live. Not in the nav at all; only in the footer.
- **Hero.** The eye-level "you cannot see your floor" shot is true and I have stood exactly there. "You find out from a screen, not a walk." Implied outcome. Does the walk go away, or get shorter, measured how? I let it pass as a description, barely.
- **Find it.** "The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." Yes. That is a spaghetti diagram in words, and I liked it. "when it was last seen and how long it has stood there". Last seen is honest. Stood there since when? Since it entered what?
- **Ask your floor.** "Which trolleys have waited at inspection for more than an hour?" The best question on any of the three pages for my job. A zone, a threshold, a unit. Picture problem: one blue trolley leaves the inspection table while the two amber ones that have waited longer stay. Either that is a FIFO break, which is a story, or it is careless. I noticed. MCP again, unknown.
- **How it knows.** Readable. Battery "1 to 3 years, depending on use" is qualified properly. "Times every wait." Three words for the metric I care most about, with no definition. Read twice: "This is spatial intelligence: the exact place and time of everything that moves in your plant, joined with jobs and zones." (22 words). "Exact" two paragraphs after "±10 cm depending on your site". "Everything that moves", when only tagged things are tracked. "Joined with jobs" with no word on who joins them or how. Three things I do not trust in one sentence. Also "Sends a short pulse when it moves and sleeps when it stops". So when the trolley is waiting, the tag is asleep. How does the system know it is still there and not carried off? I need "last observed" spelled out here, because a wait is exactly the moment the tag goes quiet.
- **Start with one bay.** "Each stage has a check before the next, and you can stop at any of them." The closest any concept comes to stages of measurement. But what is the check? Against what baseline? Undefined gate. "Which walk would you cut first?" is a good line and I would use it in a kaizen kickoff.
- **Footer.** "Industrial engineering: How waits are timed". Good, and only here.

### 3. Where I would stop
At "How it knows", on "the exact place and time of everything that moves". That sentence tells me marketing wrote the paragraph, and I start reading the rest as a quiz.

### 4. Question I still have
"What does 'times every wait' mean: from zone entry to zone exit, from the last movement, or from the last pulse? And what is the check between pilot stages?"

### 5. Score
**5/10.** Good for my supervisor, the best question for me, the weakest definitions. I would not forward it to my production manager. He would forward it to stores.

## Concept C: one floor from five heights

### 1. After the hero only
"Spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses: it shows where every tagged trolley, pallet and tool is, and how long it has waited."
Right, and the word "tagged" is in it, which I respect. The split-floor picture says "visible versus not". Fine.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "Start small" is a good label. Again, no "how waits are timed" anywhere in nav or footer. My page is gone.
- **Hero.** Same floor, two halves. I got it in one look. The queue lane at inspection is drawn as a queue lane. Good.
- **Down to the trolley.** "Down to ±10 cm, with AmIMo anchors. Your site sets the final figure." Correct. "Tags sleep when still" is said honestly, and raises the same question as B: what does the map show for a sleeping tag in a queue?
- **Over the whole floor.** "Every zone entry gets a time." Read twice: "Every zone entry gets a time. You see how long a trolley sat at inspection, or a pallet in a staging lane." (22 words). An entry alone gives no duration. You need the exit, or the time now for an open visit. A got this right with "into and out of". C dropped half of it. "Why it waited stays your team's call." Good. The rise from photo to map is the nicest picture idea for me: it is literally going from gemba to value stream map. But the end frame is a trail map with no time window.
  Count check: the hero puts three trolleys on amber in the queue and then the hero trolley joins them. Screen 3 says "three trolleys at the inspection queue". Screen 4 says three plus the hero trolley. Three or four? If I can count it on the picture, the picture has to agree with itself.
- **Ask your floor.** Same good question as B. Read twice: "AmIHub keeps the place and time of everything tagged, joined with your zones, so people, your systems and AI assistants read the same floor." (24 words). I got it on the second read. "Physical AI" I skimmed past.
- **Start with one bay.** "When it earns its place, it grows across the plant." Earns its place measured how, against what baseline? Then a list of tech facts. "Talks to MES, ERP and WMS over REST, MQTT and WebSocket" is the nearest anyone comes to "can I export the raw events", and I liked that, but it sits in the pilot screen where I am trying to read the pilot.
- **Footer.** A plain index. No row for my role.

### 3. Where I would stop
I would not stop, but I would slow down at Screen 5. It turns from a pilot into a datasheet.

### 4. Question I still have
"Where is the exit event? And what does 'earns its place' mean in numbers I agree before the pilot starts?"

### 5. Score
**6/10.** Best pictures for my head, half a definition, no page for my role.

## 6. Ranking

**A, C, B.**
A is the only one that times a move into and out of a zone, draws a queue as a queue, lets me set the zones and limits, and sends me by name to "How waits are timed".
C has the best picture logic, from floor to map, but its definition has no exit and it dropped my page.
B is the warmest story and has the best question for my job, but "times every wait" and "the exact place and time of everything that moves" are the two lines I would hand back first.

## 7. Best and worst line

**Best:** "Each move into and out of a zone gets a time." (A, Screen 2). An entry event, an exit event, in nine plain words. Runner up: "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is." (A).

**Worst:** "This is spatial intelligence: the exact place and time of everything that moves in your plant, joined with jobs and zones." (B, Screen 4). "Exact" against ±10 cm site-dependent, "everything" against tagged only, and "joined with jobs" with no association named.

## 8. One change that makes A a 10

Add one line to "Start with one bay", after "A pilot watches one flow in one bay or line":

> "The first two weeks set the baseline: how long work waits in each zone today, with visits still open at shift end counted, not dropped."

That one line gives me the baseline, puts the measurement before the "before and after", and answers the shift-end question. Then I can build the pilot scorecard from the page, and I would forward the whole page to my production manager today. (It has to be true first. The Truth and Quality Lead and the founders need to confirm that this is how AmIHub counts an open visit.)
