# Reader panel, round 1: Group Operations Reader (P8, expansion sponsor)

**SIMULATION.** This is a hypothesis buyer from kit 09 section 6, read by the panel. No real interviews sit behind it.

I read this the way I read every vendor site: on my phone, in the car between plant two and the steering committee. I have seen fifty of these. I am looking for three things: the category in one line, a story that repeats across my eleven plants, and an AI line I can take to the board without it blowing up on me. I read only section 3 (screens) and section 4 (nav).

---

## Concept A: "Where work is. Where it waits." (scan gap)

### 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "AmIMotion is spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses: tags on what moves, anchors on the columns, one live map of where everything is and how long it has stood still."
Is that right? Yes. It is the cleanest category statement of the three. It names the parts without naming the products, and "spatial intelligence" leads. No Physical AI in the opening. Good.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "Platform · Uses · Pilot · Learn · Company". Platform tells me there is a system, which is what I want. "Uses" I do not trust as a word. It reads as a translation. Fine otherwise.
- **Hero.** Calm and confident. The before/after film is the right idea, but it runs in time, so on a phone I get the copy first and the film below, and I may never see the "after". The copy carries it anyway.
- **Screen 2, "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is."** This is a line I can put on a board slide tomorrow. It respects the MES we already paid for and names the gap. Block 3, "The whole plant on one map", is the one for me. Then it ends on "One team in India designs, builds and supports every part." That sentence is in the wrong block. I was reading about flow and suddenly I am reading about the vendor. Read twice (11 words).
- **Screen 3, Ask your floor.** Good. The brochure question, a person approves, own access per assistant, every call logged, runs offline. It reads as a described capability, not a promise. It is AMBER and it behaves as AMBER, because the call on screen 5 offers the demo. Read twice: "The server runs where AmIHub runs, even on a PC in your plant with no internet." (16 words). "Where AmIHub runs" assumes I already know where AmIHub runs. I got it on the second pass.
- **Screen 4, "One team builds every layer."** Wrong headline for me. I do not buy "one team". I buy "one system", and "one team" is the vendor talking about itself. The body is exactly what I need, though: "Then it shares them with your MES, ERP, WMS and AI assistants." That is the one-data-model claim. "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications" is here, as charter wording. Good. "Runs in automotive, robotics and warehousing plants." I do not trust this until somebody shows me one. If it is not confirmed live, cut it. A fake reference kills a group rollout faster than no reference.
- **Screen 5, Start with one bay.** Bounded, clear, MES stays in charge. "Then grow plant-wide on the same AmIHub." It stops at the plant wall. My question is plant two, and this page has no answer for it.
- **Footer.** It routes "Group digital teams" to "What is spatial intelligence". That is a definition page. I already know the definition. I want "how it scales across sites". The entity paragraph is right on Physical AI: it appears once, as direction ("the first layer of"), and is not shown as delivered. But it says "spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses. AmIMotion builds industrial spatial intelligence." That is the same idea twice in two sentences. Read twice (paragraph, about 95 words).

Words I did not trust: "Uses", "Runs in" (the unconfirmed sites).

### 3. Where I would stop
I did not stop. I slowed down at screen 4, where the headline turned to the vendor.

### 4. My question at the end
"What does plant two cost me, and who runs this at group level?" Nothing on the page answers it.

### 5. Score: 6 / 10
It has the category, the standards line and an honest MCP story. It has no scale story beyond one plant, and the system screen is headlined about the team, not the system.

## Concept B: "The buyer's morning"

### 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "AmIMotion puts a tag on trolleys, bins and tools and shows them on a live map, so my supervisors stop walking the floor."
Is that right? Half right. The category is in the copy, but the hero is written for my plant head, not for me. "You find out from a screen, not a walk" is a shift-supervisor benefit. I could not repeat a category to my board from this. I could repeat a time saving.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "How it works · Where it helps · Your floor · Company". "Your floor": singular. I have eleven floors. The label tells me who this site is for, and it is not me.
- **Hero.** The camera rising past an anchor on a column is a beautiful idea and it shows the hardware in context. But the film plays once and holds, and I am on a phone.
- **Screen 2, Find it.** A real AmIHub screen capture: finally, proof, not a render. I trust this screen the most of the three concepts. The voice is plant-floor ("The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk."). Good for P4. For me it is a point solution until I see the model behind it.
- **Screen 3, Ask your floor.** The question has moved to the WIP door. Good. But this line worries me: "AmIHub has a built-in MCP server, so the assistant your team already uses can connect." Read twice (17 words). The assistant my team already uses may not speak MCP. That is a compatibility promise the file does not make. The right wording is "any assistant that speaks MCP can connect." "We show it live on the call" is the honest AMBER qualifier, and I like it.
- **Screen 4, "Every trolley tells the room where it is."** Read twice (8 words). "Tells the room" means nothing to me. The three part blocks are clear and specific: 3 to 8 m, power only, ±10 cm depending on site, PC or cloud. Then the category paragraph is buried at the bottom of the fourth screen. "The exact place and time of everything that moves in your plant." Everything? Only what is tagged. That is an overclaim, and my IT head will catch it. "Joined with jobs and zones": jobs need an association step, and the paragraph does not say so. Physical AI appears once, as "first layer". That is the right form, in the wrong place, under the hardware.
- **Screen 5, "Start with one bay. Grow to the plant."** "Each stage has a check before the next, and you can stop at any of them." Good for a promoter. "Which walk would you cut first?" is a sales question for a plant head, and it does nothing for me.
- **Footer.** It routes "Digital and group operations: Spatial intelligence" to the definition page again. "Integrators: Partners" goes to a partner page with no partners that can be named. That is an empty room.

Words I did not trust: "tells the room", "everything that moves", "the assistant your team already uses", "Partners".

### 3. Where I would stop
At screen 4's headline. If I had a plant visit coming up, I would close the tab there. The category paragraph below it is the one thing I came for, and it comes too late.

### 4. My question at the end
"Is this a plant tool or a group platform?" B makes it a plant tool.

### 5. Score: 5 / 10
It is the best page in the set for my plant heads, and I would forward it to them. It is the weakest for me: plant-singular, an MCP compatibility overclaim, the category buried, and no repeat story.

## Concept C: "One floor, seen from five heights"

### 1. After the hero only
Say-back: "AmIMotion is spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses: it shows where every tagged trolley, pallet and tool is, and how long it has waited."
Is that right? Yes. The category comes first, and "tagged" is honest. The split-frame hero (same floor, two ways, divided by a real aisle line) works in one still. I do not have to wait for an "after". On a phone, that matters more than anything else on the screen. The sub-line opens with a fragment, "Spatial intelligence for factories and warehouses.", but it reads as a label, and that is fine.

### 2. Screen by screen
- **Nav.** "How it works · Where it helps · Start small · Company". "Start small" is the most honest label in any of the three navs, and it tells me the pilot is bounded. These are buyer words, not vendor words.
- **Hero.** One frame, two meanings. I could lift this picture straight into a steering deck as the "why".
- **Screen 2, "Your trolleys say where they are."** Three parts, one sentence each, then accuracy, battery and cables in AMBER wording. It puts specs early, and for me that is right: it tells me this team is not hiding the plumbing.
- **Screen 3, the rise.** This is the zoom-out story I came for: one trolley, then the bay, then the whole plant, with stores and docks under one roof. It is the only concept that shows factory and warehouse on one model in one picture. It still stops at one plant, but it shows one model serving more flows, which is half my repeat test. "Why it waited stays your team's call" is honest.
- **Screen 4, Ask your floor + category.** Read twice, and worth it: "This is spatial intelligence. AmIHub keeps the place and time of everything tagged, joined with your zones, so people, your systems and AI assistants read the same floor." (29 words). It is long, but it is the one-data-model claim in plain words, and it is the line I would put on a board slide. Physical AI appears once, right after it, as "the first layer". That is direction, not a delivered integration. MCP: own access, every call logged, a person approves. It is AMBER, and the demo is offered on screen 5 ("an AI query, on real AmIHub data"). It passes. I would like "demo on request" closer to the MCP line itself.
- **Screen 5, Start with one bay.** "Built on the FiRa and omlox specifications. Talks to MES, ERP and WMS over REST, MQTT and WebSocket." These are open interfaces, named. That is the no-lock-in proof without saying "no lock-in". "When it earns its place, it grows across the plant on the same AmIHub." Again, the plant wall. "Runs in automotive manufacturing, robotics and warehousing." It is the same unconfirmed line as A. Cut it unless it is confirmed live. The screen is dense (seven lines before the buttons), but it is the screen I would screenshot.
- **Footer.** There is no "for your role" routing, so there is no door marked for group digital. I lose the one page meant for me. It is a plain index, which is fine for crawlers.

Words I did not trust: "Runs in" (unconfirmed). Nothing else.

### 3. Where I would stop
I did not stop. Screen 3 is where I leaned in.

### 4. My question at the end
"Plant two: same AmIHub, same zone template, and how much effort from my team?" C gets me closest to asking it, but it does not answer it either.

### 5. Score: 7 / 10
It has the cleanest arc: hardware collects, software decides, one floor, one model, people, systems and AI all read it, open specs and interfaces named, and no roadmap leaking into the present tense. I would forward it to one plant head as a candidate lighthouse. It is not yet a 10, because the scale story stops at one plant.

## 6. Ranking: C, A, B
- **C** is the only one that shows one model serving more flows (factory and warehouse in one frame), says that people, systems and AI read the same data, and names open interfaces. That is my repeat-and-no-lock-in test.
- **A** has the sharpest board line ("Scans say where it was…") and the cleanest MCP screen, but it headlines the system screen about the team, and its scale story ends at "plant-wide".
- **B** is the best page for my plant heads and the worst for me. It is plant-singular, it overclaims MCP compatibility, and the category paragraph is buried under the hardware.

## 7. Best and worst lines
- **Best:** "AmIHub keeps the place and time of everything tagged, joined with your zones, so people, your systems and AI assistants read the same floor." (C, screen 4). That is a whole steering-deck slide in one line, and it is true. A close second: "Scans say where it was. Tags say where it is." (A, screen 2).
- **Worst:** "AmIHub has a built-in MCP server, so the assistant your team already uses can connect." (B, screen 3). It promises compatibility with whatever assistant we use, and the file supports only "any MCP-capable client". Right wording: "AmIHub has a built-in MCP server. Any assistant that speaks MCP can connect. We show it live on the call." Dishonourable mention: "Every trolley tells the room where it is." (B, screen 4). I read it twice and still do not know what room.

## 8. One change that would make C a 10
Add one sentence to screen 5 that crosses the plant wall, and route group buyers to it: "The next bay, the next plant: same tags, same AmIHub, same zones you already defined." Link it to a page that answers "what does plant two cost us in effort, and who runs it at group level". **Truth flag:** the file supports "one bay, then scale on the same AmIHub" [B8] within a plant. Multi-site on one AmIHub, and reusable zone templates, need a source from the founders before this ships. If there is no source, write it as the pilot's next question ("Planning more than one plant? Ask us how the second one starts."), not as a capability. Also confirm or cut "Runs in automotive manufacturing, robotics and warehousing."
