## 1. After the hero only

Say-back: "They put a small tag on every trolley, bin and tool, anchors on the columns pick it up, and a live map tells me where it is and how long it has been waiting, so nobody walks the floor to find out."

Is it right? Yes. The hero has not changed since v3 and it still works in under ten seconds without the word "platform". "Spatial intelligence" is still the first noun and still means nothing to me, but the next four sentences do the work, so I don't stop on it. "You find out from a screen, not a walk." is my morning.

## 2. Screen by screen

- **Nav.** "How it works · Where it helps · Start small · About us · Book a call". Plain. "Start small" still tells me the risk is small. Nothing to add.
- **Hero, "Where work is. Where it waits."** Same as v3. Quiet, no numbers, no "AI" shouting. Good.
- **Screen 2, "A scan tells you where it last passed. A tag tells you where it stopped."** Still the best screen. "Last passed" is a touch better: that is exactly what my MES shows me, the last scan, hours old. "The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." is still my 7:40. "On a screen or on your phone" helps, because my supervisors live on their phones, not at a PC.
  - Nothing read twice.
- **Screen 3, "See where work waits."** Stronger than v3. "Supervisors see each wait live, during the shift, not at the next morning's review." Still the line that hits. "Set a limit for each zone" is new and it is what I would actually do: inspection, one hour, done. "Its wait becomes the job's wait." Five words, and that is the sentence that makes this about my plan versus actual, not about trolleys.
  - Read twice: "When a trolley passes it, AmIHub sends the event to the system you name." Passes what? The zone or the limit? Two sentences earlier "passes" was a scan point on screen 2. I went back to "Set a limit" to find what "it" was. Say "When a trolley waits past the limit".

- **Screen 4, "Ask your floor a question."** "Which trolleys have waited at inspection for more than an hour?" is a question I would actually ask. "Asking changes nothing. Any change... needs a supervisor's approval." Good, and "supervisor" is better than v3's "a person". I skipped the IT block. It is marked as not for me and costs me nothing.
  - Nothing read twice. Word I don't need: "MCP" (in the small block).
- **Screen 5, "Three parts work as one system."** I read the headline and the tag block. "Screws or sticks onto the trolley" is good: no drilling my carriers is a question my maintenance head will ask. "AmIHub shows each battery's level" answers my "who changes 800 batteries and when". The anchor block is 111 words now. I did not read it and I won't; that is my OT engineer's block. "Runs on a PC inside your plant, with no internet needed" is the line I would quote to my IT head.

  - Won: "The pilot times today's waits first, over a window you choose. That is your baseline." That fixes my worst line from v3. Two short sentences, read once, and I can repeat it: my own floor's number, my window, before anything else.

In v3 I said I would rather it stopped at "not people". Now it is two sentences longer, it says "consent", and it sits on the screen where I decide. ## 7. Best and worst lines

- **Best:** "Supervisors see each wait live, during the shift, not at the next morning's review." (screen 3). My meeting, my supervisor, my lost day. Close second: "Its wait becomes the job's wait."
- **Worst:** "When a trolley passes it, AmIHub sends the event to the system you name." (screen 3). The only line I read twice, "it" points at the wrong thing, and "the system you name" sounds like it is dodging the question of who finds out.

## 8. One change that would make it a 10

Put the stop on screen 6, in floor words, after "That is your baseline.": something like "Two checks before you go further: the map matches a walk of the bay with our engineer, and your team uses it at the morning meeting. If either fails, you stop there." If the founders cannot sign gates yet, at least say "You can stop after the bay." And cut the people-tagging paragraph to its first sentence. The stop alone takes it to 9. Both together make it a 10.
