# Reader panel, round 1 v4: Partner and EHS reader (P12 integrator, P10 EHS)

SIMULATION. This is a hypothesis buyer from kit 09 section 6. No interviews behind it.

I read it cold on my phone twice, once in each voice. **Integrator:** what is standard, who commissions it, who supports it, where are the docs, and can I sell it again? **EHS:** does it track people, where is the line drawn, and who draws it?

I read only section 3 (the screens) and section 4 (the nav and footer). I skipped the claim tables, the "lands for" lines and the build notes.

## 1. After the hero only

**Say-back:** "You put a small tag on each trolley, bin and tool, anchors on the columns pick up its signal, and one live map shows where it went and how long it has waited, so you check a screen instead of walking the floor."

**Is that right?** Yes, for both voices, and it's the same as v3 because the hero hasn't changed. The integrator gets three parts and a column mount. The EHS lead gets a list of things with no person in it. The hero still doesn't say "not people", but screen 2 says it fifteen seconds later, so that's fine.

## 2. Screen by screen

- **Nav.** Same as v3 for me. "People and privacy: Carriers, not people" is still under About us, which is the right label in the wrong drawer. Safety people don't look under About us. "For developers: API and MCP docs" names docs. There's still no Partners or Integrators entry. "How to buy: Call, NDA pack, survey, pilot" lays out four steps, and none of them is mine as an integrator.
- **Screen 1, Hero.** Clean. No people, no safety hook. Nothing to object to.
- **Screen 2, Find it.** "It goes on the trolley, not on the person pushing it." Still the best EHS line on the site, and it's still early. "The walk. The calls to stores. The second walk." Any shift lead will nod at that. "Look it up... on your phone." The integrator notes a mobile view to set up. Nothing to re-read.
- **Screen 3, See where work waits.** The integrator likes this screen better than v3's: "AmIHub sends the event to the system you name" and "in AmIHub or from your own system" are interface work I can quote. **Read twice:** "When a trolley passes it, AmIHub sends the event to the system you name." (13 words). "Passes it" first read as the trolley physically passing the zone. On the second read I saw it means passing the limit. The EHS voice paused again at "Supervisors see each wait live, during the shift". It's a touch stronger than v3's "can see". A works committee member hears "live" and "supervisor" together. "Each wait" and "Its wait becomes the job's wait" bring it back to the trolley, and so do the amber trolleys in the picture. It holds, but it's the closest the page gets to the line.
- **Screen 4, Ask your floor.** "Asking changes nothing." "Any change... needs a supervisor's approval." "Each assistant gets its own access." "Every request it makes is logged." Plain boundaries. As EHS I'm content. The question is about trolleys, not people.
- **Screen 5, One system.** Still the integrator's best screen, and better than v3. "You set anchors up from a phone app." "AmIHub finds each one on its own." That's a commissioning step I can picture doing on site. "Needs only power, from DC or PoE." "3 to 8 m up." "We survey your floor and set the count." I can scope a quote from this. "Omlox hub interfaces" is named. **Read twice:** "Times each pulse as it arrives, and the angle it came from." (11 words). You time an arrival, but you don't time an angle. The verb doesn't carry both halves. Still no interface version anywhere. "Raises an alert if one goes dark" made the EHS voice stop for half a second at "alert", but it's about an anchor, so it's fine. "±10 cm" is fine on a trolley. Nobody needs to think about it on a badge, because the badge isn't in scope.
- **Screen 6, Start with one bay.**
  - **EHS:** "The pilot tags trolleys, bins and pallets, not people. Tagging people would be a separate project. It would need its own policy and the consent of the people tagged." That is the line. "Its own policy" and "consent of the people tagged" give the pen to the people being tagged, not to management. "Would" keeps it off the pilot and off today. And the "People and privacy" link now sits right under it, not just in the footer. My v3 worst line is gone. A works committee can read this paragraph aloud and find nothing to use against it.
  - **Integrator:** "One contract and one support line cover every layer. Your team or your integrator can build on it at any layer, through open interfaces." The awkward "One team owns every layer" is gone. That's better. But read together with "our survey", "local engineers on your site" and the phone-app commissioning on screen 5, the split I'm left with is this: the vendor surveys, installs and supports, and I build on top. That's an honest answer to half my question. It still never says who commissions when I bring the client, who supports what I build after handover, or what a standard install contains as opposed to what's configurable.
  - **Read twice:** "Before it starts, we agree with you how a wait still open at shift end counts." (16 words). "A wait still open... counts" knots up on a phone. It's the same kind of knot as v3's baseline line, only moved one sentence along.
  - "A second plant joins the same console, with its own survey." The integrator hears repeat business, and a survey he could be paid for. Good.
  - "Prices and pilot options are shared under NDA, after the first call." No channel economics on the page. Good.
  - "Led by Kaushal Kukkar, founder and CEO, and Vivek Dham, CTO." Named people on the call, not "our team". The integrator trusts that more than a form.
- **Footer.** "Safety and EHS: People and privacy" is right. "Developers and integrators: For developers" is unchanged from v2 and v3. I'm still filed with developers and sent to API docs, not to a partner path. The entity paragraph uses carrier wording throughout. **Word I don't trust:** "Physical AI". It's once, in the footer. It still says nothing to either of my roles.

## 5. Score: **9/10**

The EHS half now passes my whole test. Within 90 seconds I can say the pilot tags carriers and not people, and that tagging people needs its own policy and the consent of the people tagged. No scene or line implies people tracking, and safety isn't sold as a feature. I'd forward it to the plant head today with no objection. The integrator half fails in the same place it failed in v3: I still can't say what is standard or who commissions and supports it. Two lines were read twice, and my test wants zero.

## 6. Compared with v3 for me (8/10)

**Better.** The people line now has "its own policy" and "the consent of the people tagged", which was the exact change I asked for, and the privacy link sits right under it. Screen 5's phone-app setup and auto-discovery give the integrator a real commissioning picture.
But the commissioning and support split is still missing. The footer still files integrators under developers, and screen 3's "live" for supervisors brings the page a step closer to the people line.

## 7. Best and worst lines

**Best:** "Tagging people would be a separate project. It would need its own policy and the consent of the people tagged." (Screen 6.) It draws the line plainly and hands the pen to the right people. I said nearly the same about "It goes on the trolley, not on the person pushing it." in v3, and that line still stands too.

**Worst:** "Your team or your integrator can build on it at any layer, through open interfaces." (Screen 6.) It's not wrong. It names me and then tells me nothing I can sell: no standard package, no commissioning owner, no support split. For an integrator, "build on it at any layer" sounds like custom work, and custom work is where my margin goes to die.

## 8. One change that would make it a 10

On screen 6, after "One contract and one support line cover every layer", add one sentence that names the split. For example: "We survey and commission the pilot. Your integrator can extend it on our versioned interface docs, and we support every layer we ship." Then change the footer row to "Integrators" pointing at a partner page that says what a standard install contains. Keep developers on their own row.

That wording is an open question for the Truth and Quality Lead and the founders. Nothing I read confirms who commissions under a partner, whether integrators may hold first-line support, or that the docs are versioned. Name no partner and show no logo until that's settled. Fixing the two re-read lines would close the rest of my test.
